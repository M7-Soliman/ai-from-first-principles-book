Part CFilters, phase and the window

What a filter is, which half of a spectrum carries the structure, and the trade that makes a spectrogram a compromise rather than a picture. The last of these is a genuine impossibility, and it is the reason every audio system has a window length in its configuration.

§17 A filter is a convolution

Go and read

Oppenheim & Schafer, Discrete-Time Signal Processing. The standard reference for movements A to C, and where to go when you need a filter designed rather than explained. Chapter 4 on sampling is the one to read first.

One object, two descriptions

A linear time-invariant system is completely described by its impulse response (what it outputs when fed a single spike) and its output on any input is that response convolved with the input.

By §14 it is equally described by its frequency response, the transform of the impulse response, which says what it does to each frequency: a magnitude that scales and a phase that delays. These are the same object in two bases, and which one to think in is decided entirely by which question is being asked.

§18 Magnitude and phase

Go and read

Oppenheim & Lim (1981), The importance of phase in signals. The magnitude-and-phase swap done properly; Figure 4 reproduces it. Read it before you design any loss that compares spectra, because what it establishes is what such a loss cannot see.

Why the structure is in the phase

Magnitude says how much of each frequency is present. Phase says where each one is: where its crests fall. An edge is many frequencies whose crests line up at one place, and it is that alignment, not the amounts, that makes the edge.

Which reframes several things at once. It is why §27's power spectrum is nearly the same for all natural images and tells you almost nothing about which image you have. It is why a loss that compares magnitudes only (a spectral loss in an audio model) will happily accept output that sounds wrong. And it is a caution about a whole style of analysis: a magnitude spectrum is a summary that discards the part carrying the content.

§19 Frequency response, and the four kinds

Named by what they keep

Low-pass keeps low frequencies. This is a blur, an average, a smoother, and §6's anti-aliasing filter.

High-pass keeps high frequencies. A difference, a sharpener. The Laplacian kernel $[1,-2,1]$ is high-pass, with its largest response exactly at Nyquist.

Band-pass keeps a range. A Gabor filter is band-pass and oriented, which is what Part X §31's pitfall box reports emerging in a trained first layer, alongside sparse coding and ICA.

All-pass changes only phase. It sounds useless and is not: it is how a delay is implemented, and by §18 it can change an image beyond recognition while leaving its power spectrum identical.

Pitfall: the centred difference is not a high-pass filter

The symmetric derivative kernel $[-1,0,1]$, which is what sits inside every Sobel operator, has response $|2\sin\theta|$: exactly zero at DC, and exactly zero at Nyquist, peaking halfway between. It is band-pass.

So it is blind to precisely the alternating pattern a high-pass filter is supposed to catch: a checkerboard at the sampling limit passes through it untouched. Drill C11 measures this, and it is a good example of a label carried over from intuition that the frequency response contradicts in one line.

The binomial kernels of §6 have the mirror-image property: exact zeros at Nyquist, which is precisely what an anti-aliasing filter wants and is why they are the cheap default.

§20 Two ways to build one

FIR and IIR

Finite impulse response. Output is a weighted sum of the last $k$ inputs. Always stable, can have exactly linear phase (meaning every frequency is delayed equally, so nothing is smeared relative to anything else) and needs many taps for a sharp cutoff. Every convolutional layer in this book is FIR.

Infinite impulse response. Output depends on previous outputs as well. Far cheaper for a sharp response, can be unstable, and cannot have linear phase. This is a recurrence, and a linear recurrent layer is an IIR filter, which is the connection Part XI §11's repeated matrix and the state space models of §35 both live on.

§21 Leakage, and the window

The seam, and what to do about it

§10's pitfall: the DFT treats your record as one period of a periodic signal, so unless the ends match there is a discontinuity, and a discontinuity spreads energy across every bin. A pure tone that does not complete a whole number of cycles in the record shows up as a peak with skirts (spectral leakage) and the skirts can bury a genuine weak component nearby.

The repair is a window: multiply the record by a function tapering to zero at both ends, so there is no seam. Hann, Hamming and Blackman are the usual choices, and they differ in how they trade a wider main lobe against lower skirts. Windowing is not optional in any careful spectral measurement; the unwindowed default is a rectangular window, which is the worst of them.

§22 The uncertainty principle for signals

A limit, not a difficulty

A signal cannot be arbitrarily concentrated in time and in frequency at once. Their spreads obey $\Delta t \, \Delta f \ge c$ for a constant depending on how spread is defined, with equality for a Gaussian.

It is the same mathematical fact as the one in quantum mechanics (position and momentum are a Fourier pair) and it has nothing to do with physics. It is a property of the transform.

§23 The spectrogram, and the mel scale

Short-time analysis

A spectrogram is the magnitude of the transform of successive overlapping windowed segments, a two-dimensional array indexed by time and frequency. Every one has three parameters: window length, hop, and window function. Figure 5 is what the first of them decides.

A mel spectrogram then pools the frequency axis into bands spaced logarithmically at high frequency, following measurements of human pitch discrimination.

Pitfall: a mel spectrogram is a modelling assumption

The mel scale is a claim that frequency resolution should degrade with frequency the way human hearing's does. That is right for speech intended for people and wrong the moment the task is not perceptual: machine fault detection, animal vocalisation, radio. It also discards phase entirely, which by §18 is most of the structure, and is why reconstructing audio from a mel spectrogram needs a model to invent the phase back.

Two defaults, both inherited without argument, and Part XXX has to unpick them.

§24 What movement C establishes

Three things

A filter is one object with two descriptions, and the frequency one turns "what does this do" into a per-frequency scalar.

Phase carries the structure. Figure 4 measures a reconstruction correlating +0.736 with its phase donor and −0.125 with its magnitude donor. Any method that keeps magnitude and discards phase has discarded the content.

Time and frequency resolution cannot both be had. Figure 5 measures the product constant at 1.875 while each factor moves sixteen-fold. A spectrogram is a choice on that curve, not a picture of the signal.
