Part EContinuous time

Part XVI taught diffusion models as a ladder of discrete noise levels. The literature writes them as a stochastic differential equation, and the change is not cosmetic: it produces a second sampler that shares the first one's marginals, an exact likelihood, and a vocabulary in which the design choices become visible.

§33 Brownian motion

The object everything here is built from

A Wiener process $W_t$ starts at zero, has independent increments, and $W_{t+\Delta} - W_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Delta)$.

The consequence that matters is the scaling: displacement grows as $\sqrt{\Delta}$, not as $\Delta$. So the path is continuous and nowhere differentiable, and over a short interval it moves much further than anything with a velocity would. Every unfamiliar thing in this movement follows from that one square root.

§34 Stochastic differential equations

Drift and diffusion

$$d\mathbf{x} \;=\; \underbrace{f(\mathbf{x}, t)\,dt}_{\text{drift}} \;+\; \underbrace{g(t)\,d\mathbf{W}}_{\text{diffusion}}$$

Read as: over an instant, move deterministically by $f\,dt$ and randomly by $g$ times a Gaussian of standard deviation $\sqrt{dt}$. Simulating it by Euler–Maruyama is that sentence written as code, and the $\sqrt{\Delta t}$ on the noise term is the only thing that distinguishes it from an ODE solver.

Part XVI's forward process is one of these: $f = -\tfrac{1}{2}\beta(t)\mathbf{x}$ shrinks the data toward the origin and $g = \sqrt{\beta(t)}$ adds noise, so the distribution flows from the data to a standard Gaussian. The "noise schedule" that arrives as a mysterious list of numbers is $\beta(t)$.

§35 Itô's lemma, and why calculus changes

The chain rule acquires a term

For an ordinary function of a deterministic path, second-order terms vanish. Here they do not: $(dW)^2 = dt$ rather than something negligible, because of §33's square root. So

$$dh(\mathbf{x}_t) \;=\; \left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial t} + f\cdot\nabla h + \tfrac{1}{2}g^2 \nabla^2 h\right) dt \;+\; g\,\nabla h \cdot d\mathbf{W}$$

The extra Laplacian term is not a correction to be dropped. It is why a martingale can have a non-zero expected change under a nonlinear transformation, and, more usefully here, it is what the Fokker–Planck equation for the density is built from, which is where the next section's result comes from.

§36 The reverse-time SDE

The result the whole field rests on

A diffusion run backwards in time is another diffusion, and its drift involves the gradient of the log density:

$$d\mathbf{x} \;=\; \left[f(\mathbf{x},t) - g(t)^2\,\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x})\right] dt \;+\; g(t)\, d\bar{\mathbf{W}}$$

Everything in the bracket is known except $\nabla \log p_t$, the score. So: corrupt data with a process you designed, learn the score of the corrupted distribution at every noise level, and run the reverse process to generate. That is the whole algorithm, and Part XVI §23's score matching is how the score is learned.

§37 The probability-flow ODE

The same marginals with no noise at all

There is a deterministic equation whose marginal distribution at every time matches the SDE's:

$$\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt} \;=\; f(\mathbf{x},t) - \tfrac{1}{2}g(t)^2\,\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x})$$

Half the score term, and no noise. The paths are completely different (this one is invertible and the SDE's are not) while the distribution at each time is the same.

Three things follow. Sampling becomes deterministic given the starting noise, so a seed is a sample. The map is invertible, so an image can be encoded to its latent and back. And because it is a continuous normalising flow, the exact log-likelihood is available, which the SDE does not give.

One folk claim, corrected

The ODE is often said to need fewer steps than the SDE. Figure 7 measures the opposite with Euler integration, and the resolution is that the claim is about higher-order solvers. A deterministic equation admits them and a stochastic one does not, which is where the advantage comes from, not from removing the noise.

Read the other way, the noise is doing something: it is a correction that pulls a drifting trajectory back toward the correct marginal, and at low step counts with a crude integrator that correction is worth more than determinism.

§38 What this reframes about Part XVI

Four things that were choices and looked like conventions

The noise schedule is $\beta(t)$, and variance-preserving against variance-exploding is a choice of $f$ and $g$ rather than two separate algorithms.

The number of sampling steps is a discretisation, so a better solver buys steps directly, which is what every fast sampler in the literature is.

Guidance modifies the score, so it modifies the drift; classifier-free guidance is extrapolating between two drifts and its characteristic distortion at high strength is the extrapolation, not a training defect.

Flow matching (Part XVI §32) drops the stochastic process and regresses the ODE's velocity field directly, which is why it can use straighter paths and fewer steps. Movement F's transport is where those straight paths come from.

§39 What movement E establishes

Three things

Noise scales as the square root of time, and every unfamiliar thing in stochastic calculus, including Itô's extra term, is that fact.

A diffusion reversed is a diffusion whose drift needs the score. That single result is what makes generative diffusion possible, and Part XVI's discrete ladder is its discretisation.

The same marginals admit a deterministic sampler, which buys invertibility and an exact likelihood, and Figure 7 measures the honest cost: with a first-order solver it is less accurate, not more.
