§7 The same operation, one sequence

In 60 seconds

Let the queries, keys and values all come from the same sequence. Every position now builds its own summary of every other position, in one parallel operation with no recurrence anywhere. That single change removes both of the limitations cross-attention left standing.

Go and read

Vaswani et al. (2017), Attention is all you need. The title is a joke about exactly the move this section makes: the accessory turns out to be the whole thing. Read §3.2 for scaled dot-product attention, and do not skip the footnote justifying the $\sqrt{d_k}$: it is §4's variance argument, compressed into two sentences, and it is the only derivation in the paper.

In §6 the queries came from a decoder and the keys and values from an encoder. Take one sequence $X$ of $n$ positions and produce all three from it:

$$Q = XW^Q, \qquad K = XW^K, \qquad V = XW^V, \qquad \text{SelfAttn}(X) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$

Every position attends to every position, including itself. The output is $n$ vectors, one per position, each a weighted blend of all $n$ values with weights that position chose.

Shapes, and where the parallelism is

$$\underbrace{X}_{(n,\,d)} \to \underbrace{Q, K, V}_{(n,\,d)} \to \underbrace{QK^\top}_{(n,\,n)} \to \underbrace{\text{softmax}(\cdot)V}_{(n,\,d)}$$

Four matrix multiplications and a softmax. No loop over positions appears anywhere. Part XI §6's $O(\tau)$ sequential dependency is not reduced; it is absent, because $\mathbf{h}^{(t)}$ never needed $\mathbf{h}^{(t-1)}$ in the first place.

That is the property that displaced recurrence, and §21 returns to it once causal masking makes it usable for generation.

§8 Path length

Part XI §28's first limitation was that information from position 1 reaches position 1,000 through 999 applications of the same transformation. Self-attention connects them directly.

Why constant path length matters more than the raw number suggests

Part XI §11 showed that a gradient travelling $t$ steps is multiplied by $t$ Jacobians, and §12 showed the decay is inseparable from stable memory. With a path of length 1 there is no product: the gradient from position 1,000 reaches position 1 through a single softmax and a single matrix.

So the vanishing-gradient problem of Part XI Part C does not get better; it stops applying. All of Part XI §§16–21's machinery (skip connections through time, leaky units, gating, forget-gate biases) was built to manage a product of Jacobians that no longer exists.

This is worth registering as a pattern. Three parts have now met a problem caused by composing many transformations, and the durable fix each time was architectural, not algorithmic: residual connections in Part X, gating in Part XI, and here the removal of the composition altogether. Part IX §27's principle, for the third time.

§9 Permutation equivariance, and why it is a defect here

Self-attention has an exact symmetry, and this time it is bad news.

Self-attention is permutation equivariant

For any permutation matrix $P$:

$$\text{SelfAttn}(PX) = P\,\text{SelfAttn}(X)$$

Shuffle the input positions and the outputs shuffle identically. Measured at $2.4\times10^{-7}$ relative: float32 rounding, exactly as Part X §9 measured for translation.

Look at the definition and it is obvious: the score $\mathbf{q}_i^\top\mathbf{k}_j$ depends on the contents at $i$ and $j$ and on nothing else. There is no $i$ or $j$ in the formula. The softmax sums over a set. Attention does not know what order its input is in, because nothing in the operation refers to order.

Why the same property is a virtue in Part X and a defect here

Part X §9 measured translation equivariance and called it the point of the architecture. This is the same kind of statement, a symmetry the model is built to respect, and here it has to be destroyed.

The difference is whether the symmetry is true of the task. Part X §11's framing answers it: an architecture encodes an assumption, and the assumption is either right or wrong about your data. Translating a cat does not change that it is a cat. Permuting the words of a sentence does change what it means: dog bites man and man bites dog are the same multiset.

So self-attention as defined is a set operation, and language is not a set. The order information has to be put back by hand, which is what §10 and §11 are about, and it is the one place where a transformer needs something bolted on rather than derived.

§10 Putting the order back

Two families, and the difference between them turned out to matter enormously.

Absolute positional encoding

Give each position $p$ a vector $\text{PE}(p)$ and add it to the input embedding before the first layer. Two standard choices:

Learned. An embedding table indexed by position, trained like any other parameter. Simple, and it cannot extrapolate past the longest sequence it saw: position 5,000 has no entry and no way to invent one.

Sinusoidal. Fixed, not learned:

$$\text{PE}(p, 2i) = \sin\!\big(p/10000^{2i/d}\big), \qquad \text{PE}(p, 2i+1) = \cos\!\big(p/10000^{2i/d}\big)$$

A bank of sinusoids at geometrically spaced wavelengths, from about $2\pi$ to about $10000 \cdot 2\pi$. Defined for every $p$, so it extrapolates in the sense of producing something, though a model trained only on short sequences has no reason to use it well.

This is a Fourier basis evaluated at $p$, and the property that makes it work is the one Part XXVII §33 derives: a shift becomes a rotation of each sine–cosine pair, so relative position is a fixed linear function of the encoding. Every method for extending a model's context is an edit to this frequency bank.

Intuition: why sinusoids, and not just the number $p$

The obvious encoding is to append $p$ itself. Two problems, and both are instructive.

Scale. $p$ grows without bound while the embeddings are $O(1)$, so by position 1,000 the positional signal swamps the content. Normalising by the maximum length reintroduces a maximum length.

What the model needs is usually relative. "The previous word" is a useful notion at every position; "position 847" rarely is. A bank of sinusoids has a property that a raw integer does not: $\text{PE}(p + k)$ is a fixed linear function of $\text{PE}(p)$ for each offset $k$, because rotating a sine and cosine pair by a fixed angle is a linear map. So a model can learn to attend by relative offset: the representation makes it expressible.

Can, but need not. The relative structure is available and nothing enforces its use, which is exactly the gap §11 closes.

§11 RoPE

Go and read

Su et al. (2024), RoFormer: enhanced transformer with rotary position embedding. Short, and the derivation is the point: the relative-position property is established as an identity rather than encouraged by a representation. That is what distinguishes it from everything before it, and Figure 4 is the identity checked.

Rotary position embedding takes §10's observation and makes it structural: rather than adding something a model could use to infer relative position, rotate the queries and keys so that their inner product depends on relative position and nothing else.

Rotary position embedding

Split each query and key into $d/2$ pairs of coordinates and rotate pair $i$ at position $m$ by angle $m\theta_i$, with $\theta_i = 10000^{-2i/d}$, the same geometric spacing as the sinusoids.

Writing the rotation as $R_m$, the score becomes

$$\big(R_m\mathbf{q}\big)^\top\big(R_n\mathbf{k}\big) = \mathbf{q}^\top R_m^\top R_n \mathbf{k} = \mathbf{q}^\top R_{n-m}\,\mathbf{k}$$

because rotations compose by adding angles and $R_m^\top = R_{-m}$. The absolute positions cancel. The score is a function of $\mathbf{q}$, $\mathbf{k}$ and the offset $n - m$ alone.

Why this displaced the alternatives

It is a property, not a hope. Sinusoidal encoding makes relative attention expressible; RoPE makes it the only thing expressible. A model cannot accidentally learn to key off absolute position, because absolute position is not in the score.

It applies at every layer. Absolute encodings are added once, at the input, and are progressively mixed away by the layers above. RoPE is applied to the queries and keys of every attention operation, so the positional structure does not decay with depth.

It leaves the values alone. Only queries and keys are rotated: position affects where you look, not what you get back, which is the right factorisation given §3.

The caveat is that extrapolating beyond the trained length still fails, and the reason is now precise: the model has never seen the rotation angles that long offsets produce. §39 returns to it, because the fixes (interpolating the frequencies, or changing the base) are where practical long-context work actually happens.

RoPE is not the only answer to §9. ALiBi skips rotation entirely: it subtracts a penalty proportional to distance directly from the attention logit, per head, before the softmax: no vectors added to $Q$ or $K$ at all, and nothing in the penalty that was ever fit to a training length, which is why it extrapolates by construction rather than by patching.

§12 Multiple heads

One attention operation produces one weighted average per position. But a position may need several unrelated things at once (the subject of its verb, the noun its adjective modifies, the earlier mention of its pronoun) and a single softmax must choose between them.

Multi-head attention

Split the model dimension into $h$ heads of size $d_k = d/h$, run attention independently in each, concatenate, and mix with a final projection:

$$\text{MHA}(X) = \big[\text{head}_1; \ldots; \text{head}_h\big]W^O, \qquad \text{head}_i = \text{Attention}(XW_i^Q, XW_i^K, XW_i^V)$$

The parameter count is unchanged: the projections that were $d \times d$ become $h$ projections of $d \times d/h$. Heads are free; they cost only the reshaping.

Pitfall: what heads are and are not

Attention maps are seductive. They are the one part of a network that looks interpretable without any effort, and a great deal has been read into them that does not survive checking.

Heads do not carve up cleanly by function. Some do: §37's induction heads are a genuine, mechanistically understood example. Most are not one thing, and the same head often does different work in different contexts.

A head is not a unit of computation on its own. The output passes through $W^O$, which mixes all heads together, so the model's behaviour depends on head combinations. Reading a single head's map in isolation is reading one term of a sum.

Attention weights are not an explanation. §36 is about this: a position can be attended to strongly and contribute almost nothing, because the weight multiplies a value that may be near zero. High attention is neither necessary nor sufficient for high influence, and this has been demonstrated repeatedly.

What is well established: more heads help, up to a point, and the useful count grows with model size. Many heads in trained models can be pruned with little loss, which is itself informative: the redundancy is real.

§13 What it costs

The score matrix $QK^\top$ is $n \times n$. Everything follows from that.

The two terms, and where they cross

Per layer, ignoring constants:

ComputeMemory
The four projections$O(nd^2)$$O(nd)$
Scores and the weighted sum$O(n^2d)$$O(n^2)$
The feedforward layer (§15)$O(nd^2)$$O(nd)$

The quadratic term dominates once $n$ is large enough, and "large enough" is $n \approx 4d$: below that, a transformer's cost is dominated by matrix multiplications that have nothing to do with attention.

Why the crossover is the number to carry

"Attention is quadratic" is true and, on its own, misleading. At $d = 4096$ the crossover is at $n \approx 16{,}000$, so for a model with a 4,096-token context, attention is not the bottleneck, and optimising it buys little.

This explains something that otherwise looks like a puzzle: a large literature of efficient attention mechanisms with excellent asymptotics that were not adopted. Below the crossover they optimise the minority term, and they usually cost accuracy to do it. What was adopted instead, §29's FlashAttention, changes no asymptotics at all and attacks memory traffic.

The memory story is different and less forgiving. Compute can be traded against time; the score matrix either fits or it does not, and $O(n^2)$ reaches a modern accelerator's capacity around 65,000 positions for one matrix in one head of one layer. That is the wall §29 had to remove before long context was possible at all.
