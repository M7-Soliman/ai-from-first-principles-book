What to check before choosing a representation, how to evaluate systems whose metrics depend on the evaluation set's size, and which of this part's problems are architectural.

§40 Choosing a representation

Five questions, in order

Is the signal speech, and is it for people? If both, the mel spectrogram's assumption is right and the default pipeline is a good default. If either is no, §4's fourteen-fold pooling is discarding something and a linear or learned filterbank deserves a comparison.

Do you need to reconstruct? If yes, phase is on your critical path and §7 is the cost. If you only need to classify, the magnitude may genuinely be enough.

How fast does the signal change? This sets the window, and the 25 ms default is a guess about speech.

Do you need timing? A clip-level embedding cannot supply it, however good it is.

Will the output feed a language model? Then you need tokens, and §22's codec is the interface.

§41 Evaluating audio and multimodal systems

Six things

Report the gallery size with every retrieval number. Figure 7 is why.

Report the prompts for a zero-shot number, since the wording moves it by points.

Report the text-only control for any multimodal benchmark.

Report word error rate with the language model separated, because a CTC system's score is partly the external model's: Figure 4 says how much of the work it is doing.

Evaluate vocoders on something other than spectral distance, which is exactly the metric Figure 2 shows a phase-blind method optimising while sounding wrong.

Split by speaker, by recording session and by source, not at random. The same voice in train and test is the audio version of the leak Part XXV §36 measures.

§42 The failures that repeat

Five, in the order they are usually discovered

A resampling with no filter. Part XXVII §3, and it is everywhere in audio pipelines because changing sample rate is a one-line call in every library.

Train and test sharing a speaker. Reported accuracy that does not survive a new voice.

A magnitude-only loss that scores well and sounds wrong.

A retrieval number from a small gallery, compared against one from a large one.

A multimodal benchmark answered from the text. No control was run, so nobody knows.

§44 What is unsolved

Four, distinguished by kind

Not yet measured. How much of a learned front end's occasional advantage is the learning and how much is the extra compute. The comparison at matched compute is rare.

Not currently measurable. Whether a multimodal model has understood a scene or matched a caption pattern. Every benchmark we have is answerable to some degree from priors, and the text-only control bounds the problem without solving it.

Genuinely open. Representing time and identity together: who spoke, when, in overlapping speech. Separation, diarisation and recognition are still largely separate systems, and the joint problem is not solved.

Not empirical. Whether a synthesised voice may resemble a person who did not consent. That is Part XXI's subject and no audio metric touches it.

§45 What to carry forward

Five things

The standard audio representation is two assumptions and a deletion. A window length guessing how fast speech changes, a mel scale claiming resolution should degrade like hearing's (Figure 1 measures fourteen-fold pooling at the top) and phase discarded entirely.

So the pipeline needs a model to invent phase back. Figure 2 measures classical estimation converging in spectrogram space to a waveform correlation of 0.53.

CTC's independence assumption costs half the probability mass on a genuinely ambiguous input, at every length, unfixable by sharper predictions, which is why an external language model is restoring something rather than refining it.

The contrastive temperature selects how many negatives you train against. Figure 6 measures 1.1 of 512 at a low setting.

A retrieval number is a property of the gallery, and a shared embedding space need not be shared, so cross-modal thresholds must be calibrated on cross-modal pairs.

Sixty seconds

Audio is too long and too phase-variable to feed a model raw, so everything runs on a spectrogram, which builds in a guess about how fast the signal changes, a claim that frequency resolution should degrade the way human hearing's does, and the deletion of the half of the signal that carries its structure. That last one is why generating audio needs a second model just to put phase back. Recognition is an alignment problem solved by summing over alignments, and the cheapest way of doing that assumes the output symbols are independent given the audio, which costs half the probability mass whenever the audio is genuinely ambiguous. And joining two modalities with a contrastive loss gives you retrieval, a temperature that quietly decides how many negatives you are training against, a score that means different things in different directions, and no ability to say when anything happened.
