Part EVideo and tracking

A sequence of frames is not a set of images: consecutive frames are almost identical, which is both an enormous amount of redundancy to exploit and the reason a per-frame metric can be near-perfect while the system is unusable.

§33 What a video adds

Three things, and a cost

Evidence. A pedestrian ambiguous in one frame is unambiguous over ten. Accumulating that evidence is most of what tracking is for.

Motion. Velocity and acceleration are not in a single frame, and a planner needs them.

Identity. Knowing that this is the same object as before is what makes "it is slowing down" a sentence you can say.

The cost is that frames are correlated, so a random split leaks, Part XXV §36's finding in a different shape. Splitting a video dataset by frame rather than by sequence reports a system that does not exist, and it is a mistake with a long history in this field.

§34 Tracking as association

Detect, then match

Run a detector on each frame, then decide which detection continues which track. Given a cost between every track and every detection, the assignment is a bipartite matching problem solvable exactly in polynomial time, the same problem the set-prediction detectors of §11 solve inside their loss.

What the cost is made of is the design decision: overlap, distance between predicted and observed positions, appearance similarity, or a learned combination. Everything else about the tracker follows from that choice.

§35 Motion models

Predicting where it should be

Associating on observed position fails as soon as objects move appreciably between frames. Predicting each track forward and associating on the predicted position is the fix, and the standard predictor is Part XXVIII §13's Kalman filter with a constant-velocity model.

Constant velocity is wrong for anything turning or braking, and it is wrong in a way that degrades gracefully over a few frames and catastrophically over many, which §36 measures. It is also exactly the case where the filter's covariance is doing useful work: a track that has not been seen for ten frames should have a large gate, and the filter produces that number without anybody tuning it.

§36 Through occlusion

Two readings

The motion model is worth an enormous amount and costs almost nothing: a four-state linear filter against a detector with tens of millions of parameters. It is the cheapest accuracy in the stack and it is often the last thing tuned.

It has a horizon. The failure at the right of the figure is not a bug; it is the prediction's uncertainty growing until it exceeds the distance between objects. Past that point the only thing that recovers identity is appearance, which is why re-identification exists and why it matters most exactly where the geometry has stopped helping.

§37 The metrics, and what they trade

Go and read

Luiten et al. (2021), HOTA: a higher order metric for evaluating multi-object tracking. Why one tracking number is not enough, and how to decompose it into detection and association terms that separate teams can act on. A good example of a metric paper that is really an engineering paper.

Three families, and the reason there are three

Detection-weighted. MOTA sums missed objects, false positives and identity switches into one number, which is dominated by detection quality: a tracker can improve its identity handling and barely move it.

Identity-weighted. IDF1 measures how long identities are held correctly, and rewards a tracker that keeps a consistent identity even when detections are imperfect.

Balanced. HOTA decomposes into a detection term and an association term explicitly, so the two can be reported separately, which is the useful property, because they are separate engineering problems solved by separate teams.

The general point is Part XIV §33's: a single number that mixes two things you can trade against each other tells you nothing about which one to work on.

§38 End-to-end, and what it gives up

The argument, honestly stated on both sides

For. A modular stack optimises each piece against a proxy metric, and the proxies do not compose: a detector tuned for AP is not tuned for what the planner needs. Training the whole system against driving performance removes every interface mismatch at once, and interfaces are where systems fail.

Against. The intermediate representations are what make a system debuggable, testable against ground truth, and arguable about in a safety case. Remove them and a failure has no locus: you cannot say the perception was wrong, only that the output was.

Note what is not in dispute: geometry. Calibration, projection and triangulation are correct by construction, and a learned system must rediscover them approximately from data. The interesting middle ground keeps the geometry as structure and learns everything else, which is §31's third approach.

§39 What movement E establishes

Three things

Frames are correlated, so a per-frame split leaks and a per-frame metric misleads. Split by sequence, and measure temporal consistency separately.

A four-state linear filter buys most of the association problem. Figure 8 measures 100% misidentification without one and 0.0% with one, over most of the range.

It has a horizon set by uncertainty against object spacing, and past it only appearance helps.
