Colophon

How this book was made, and how it stays correct as it grows.

§ The build

Nothing here was typeset by hand. The text lives in structured HTML source files; a Node build script assembles them, runs KaTeX server-side to typeset the mathematics, and hands the result to Chrome headless, which prints to PDF. Figures are generated by matplotlib scripts that perform the computation each figure depicts.

StageWhat happens
FiguresA Python script runs real computations (gradient descent iterations, eigenvalue decompositions, measured timing results) and emits vector graphics. Nothing is drawn by hand.
MathematicsKaTeX typesets every formula at build time and embeds the result as HTML, so the output never depends on a reader's installed fonts or a runtime renderer.
AssemblySection files carry a four-digit prefix; the prefix is the book's order. Inserting a new part requires no renumbering of existing files.
PrintingChrome headless renders the assembled document to PDF. The cover is printed at zero margins in a separate pass and spliced in by the build script, because a full-bleed cover is impossible in a single Chrome print.

§ The guards

Every check below exists because it caught a real error in this book. They are listed because they are the reason to trust what you have been reading.

Formula parse errors fail the build
A malformed KaTeX expression stops the build rather than shipping as a red error box. Caught one mis-escaped comparison operator.
Broken figure links fail the build
Caught an entire part's figures silently missing after a directory move.
Unrendered notation fails the build
Mathematical source that is never processed by KaTeX would appear as raw markup in the middle of a sentence. Caught two drill answers where the expression spanned a line break in the source.
Missing fonts fail the build
When the mathematical fonts fail to load, formulas silently fall back to a system serif and every large bracket renders as a broken glyph. Caught exactly that, across an entire part.
Oversized figures fail the build
An infinite axis limit once produced a figure roughly fifty feet tall, which silently injected eighty-seven blank pages. That figure depicted numbers overflowing.
Why list the failures

Because a book about mathematics should be honest about its own errors, and because every one of these was invisible in the source and obvious in the output. The lesson generalises beyond typesetting: check the artefact, not the intention. It is the same argument this book makes for gradient checking in Part 0 and for verifying claims by simulation in Part II.

One error was caught by the book's own test suite rather than by a build guard. A worked example carried rounded intermediate values, and the drill tests (written to match the text) disagreed with it in the fourth digit. The text was wrong; the tests were right. It has been corrected.

§ Verification

Each part ships a drill set with an automated test harness. Unimplemented exercises skip rather than fail, so a reader watches skips turn into passes while working through them. Every test was itself verified against a reference implementation, so a green run means the tests are correct and not merely silent.

Figures are computed rather than illustrated, with one labelled exception. Where a result is worth teaching but genuinely too expensive to reproduce here, the figure is marked Illustrative, carries no fabricated numbers, and states in its caption why it was not measured. Everything without that mark was computed by the script that accompanies its part. The build refuses to produce a book in which the distinction is unmarked.

When a figure claims that certain directions do not rotate under a matrix, the eigenvectors were computed and drawn. When one shows an optimiser diverging, those are real iterations. When a caption reports a number, that number came out of the code that drew the picture.

§ Typography

Body text is set in a transitional serif chosen for long-form reading; headings, labels and figures use a neutral grotesque. Mathematics is set in the traditional mathematical faces. The palette runs on one accent: amber for structure, with blue, green, purple and pink reserved for the callout categories, used consistently so that colour carries meaning rather than decoration.

§ On sources

All explanatory material here (the exposition, the worked examples, the figures, the drills and the projects) is original to this book. The mathematics is of course not; it belongs to everyone, and the historical notes throughout spend some time on who worked it out and what they were trying to do at the time.

No field is built alone. This book is written against the source chapters of Goodfellow, Bengio & Courville, Deep Learning (MIT Press, 2016), freely available at deeplearningbook.org. The scope comes from the source; none of the content does. A reading list of primary sources (one or two papers per major idea, each placed where its idea is developed) is in Reading the Literature immediately before this.

The PDF compilation of the Goodfellow et al. book distributed alongside the source material was prepared by Janishar Ali and published at janishar/mit-deep-learning-book-pdf. This project is a fork of that work; all learning material here is original.

Historical details are drawn from the standard accounts. Where a story is disputed or has likely improved in the retelling (the naming of entropy is the clearest case), the text says so rather than presenting it as settled.

§ The path the book took

The foundations, the theory of learning from data, and the general machinery of training came first: mathematics, linear algebra, probability, numerical computation, the framework, statistics, machine learning, neural networks, regularisation and optimisation. Parts X to XII extended that treatment to convolutional models, sequence models, and attention and transformers. Part XIII turned to the data itself, Part XIV to the practice of diagnosing a system that is not working, Part XV to the representation in the middle of one, Part XVI to learning a distribution rather than a function, Part XVII to what happens when you make it bigger, Part XVIII to learning from consequences you caused, Part XIX to making it cheap enough to use, Part XX to what next-token prediction turned into, Part XXI to what we can see inside these systems, what we can ask of them and what we can prove, and Part XXII to the difference between predicting and intervening, Part XXIII to why learning works at all and where the guarantees run out, Part XXIV to saying how much you do not know, Part XXV to the models that win where the data is a table, and Part XXVI to the symmetries a table and an image do not have, Part XXVII to the foundation four of those parts had been borrowing, Part XXVIII to what you can compute once a model is written as a distribution, Part XXIX to what a camera can and cannot tell you, Part XXX to the other sensor and what happens when two of them meet, Part XXXI to acting in a world that pushes back, and Part XXXII to the machine underneath and the adversary in front, and Part XXXIII to planning a route to a goal when the answer is a sequence of actions to be searched for rather than a function to be learned.

The structure was built to extend, and it did: each part is a set of numbered sections, a figure script, a drill set with tests, and its glossary entries. Nothing already written had to change when a new one arrived, and no cross-reference broke. That property is worth recording because it was the design constraint that made a book of this size writable at all.
