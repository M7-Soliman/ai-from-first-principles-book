BuildThe project

Eight to twelve hours. You take a dataset apart, plant six failures in it, and build the instruments that catch each one, then run those instruments on a dataset you did not plant anything in.

P.1 What you are building

datalab: a set of measurements that take a dataset and return numbers, plus a corpus you have deliberately corrupted in six known ways so you can prove the measurements work.

The rule: every instrument must be validated against a planted failure before you trust it on real data. A detector that has never caught anything is not evidence.

P.2 Stage 1: the instruments

Build each as a function taking a dataset and returning a number with an interval.

  1. Near-duplicate rate. Shingle, MinHash, LSH banding. Report the rate and the threshold it was measured at.
  2. Contamination. Exact match and $n$-gram overlap against a held-out set, at several $n$.
  3. Label accuracy. Sample-and-relabel, with a confidence interval (Part V §8).
  4. Agreement. Cohen's $\kappa$, overall and per class.
  5. Coverage. Counts along axes you name in advance, against a target you write down first.
  6. Diversity. Embedding dispersion or effective rank, with §18's caveat recorded in the output.

Deliverable: six functions, each with a docstring naming what it does not measure.

P.3 Stage 2: plant the failures

Take a clean dataset you generate and produce six corrupted versions, one per failure, at a severity you control:

  • A frame gap: one subgroup under-sampled by a known factor.
  • Symmetric label noise at a known rate, and asymmetric noise at the same rate.
  • Contamination: a known fraction of the test set copied in.
  • Duplicates: a subset repeated a known number of times.
  • A shortcut: a feature that predicts the label and will not in deployment.
  • Group leakage: records from one unit split across train and test.

Deliverable: for each, the answer to two questions. Does the standard metric notice?: almost always no. Does your instrument notice, and at what severity does it stop noticing? The second number is the instrument's sensitivity, and without it you do not know what a clean report means.

P.4 Stage 3: the split matters

  1. Generate data with nested structure: records within units within sites.
  2. Evaluate under a random split, a unit split and a site split.
  3. Then break the site correlation deliberately and re-evaluate all three.

Deliverable: the three numbers and one paragraph on what each is a measurement of. Then state which one you would report to someone deploying at a new site, and why the other two would be misleading rather than merely optimistic.

P.5 Stage 4: memorisation

  1. Insert canaries, random sequences you constructed, at frequencies 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32.
  2. Train a small model.
  3. Attempt extraction by prompting with a prefix, and measure exact reproduction and loss.
  4. Deduplicate so every canary appears once, retrain, and measure again.

Deliverable: your model's memorisation threshold, as a number of copies. Then the same number after deduplication, which is the argument for §21 stated in units you measured yourself.

P.6 Stage 5: collapse

  1. Fit a model to data, sample from it, refit, repeat for twenty generations.
  2. Do it three ways: plain, with a filter that keeps the most typical half, and with a model class narrower than the data.
  3. Track both the variance and something that sees structure: a mode count, a histogram distance to the original, the effective rank.

Deliverable: a demonstration that variance alone fails to detect the mismatch case. That is the finding, and it is the reason the standard monitoring does not work.

P.7 Stretch

  • Run the instruments on a real public dataset you did not construct. Report what you find, including "nothing", and say what your Stage 2 sensitivities imply about how much a null result means.
  • Write the dataset document (§19) for it, including the known-gaps section.
  • Active learning's blind spot. Implement uncertainty sampling on data with a confidently-wrong category and track whether it is ever selected.
  • The curation frontier. Train at matched compute on the full corpus and on a filtered decile, and evaluate on two evaluation sets: one resembling the filter's reference and one not.

P.8 Done when

  • Six instruments exist, each documenting what it does not measure.
  • Each has been validated against a planted failure, with a measured sensitivity.
  • You have shown a standard metric failing to notice each of the six.
  • The three splits are reported with what each measures.
  • Your memorisation threshold is a number, before and after deduplication.
  • You have demonstrated that variance does not detect collapse under model mismatch.
  • Your README names one thing you assumed about a dataset that the measurements contradicted.
Why this project

Every other project in this book builds something. This one builds the ability to disbelieve a number, which is a different and rarer skill.

Stage 2 is the core. Planting a failure and watching every standard metric report success is a specific experience, and it is not replaceable by being told that it happens. Afterwards you will look at a reported score and ask what was planted in it that nobody checked for, which is exactly the disposition Part XIV needs.

The one thing to carry forward

Scale improves precision and does nothing for validity.

Figure 1 is the part in one image: an interval of $\pm 0.003$ around an estimate wrong by 0.09. Every failure here has that shape: the number becomes tighter, more confident and more reportable while quietly ceasing to be about the thing you wanted.

So the question to carry forward is not "is this number good?" but "what is this number about?", which frame, which labels, which split, which population. The metric cannot tell you. Part XIV is about asking systematically.
