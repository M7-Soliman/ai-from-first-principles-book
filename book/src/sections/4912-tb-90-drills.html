PracticeDrills

Forty-one drills. The harness in parts/25-tabular/drills/ covers twenty of them with twenty-five tests. Numpy only: building the splitting rule is most of the point, and the book has been citing results about trees for nineteen parts without teaching one.

Tier 0 Estimate first

EstimateE1 · What binning saves

A million rows and a hundred features, split-searched exactly, against a histogram with 64 bins. Write down the ratio of candidate splits evaluated per node.

Answer. 6,400 against 108, a factor of 15,625. That single ratio is why gradient boosting is practical at scale, and Figure 5 of Part XXV measures the other half of the trade: accuracy improves up to a point, because fewer thresholds means fewer chances to fit noise. Score yourself: within a factor of two is a pass, and an order of magnitude out is worth understanding before you go on.

Tier 1 Warm-up

Warm-upW1 · Why not a convolution

A table's columns can be reordered without changing the problem. What does that rule out?

Answer. Every architectural prior of Parts X to XII. There is no locality and no translation to be equivariant to, which is why the family that works is the one whose prior is axis-aligned thresholds.
Warm-upW2 · Read Figure 1

Where does the test error bottom, and what is the training error at depth 16?

Answer. Depth 3, and 0.071 with 1,572 leaves. The classical bias-variance curve, visible because a tree's capacity is a number you can count.
Warm-upW3 · Deep or shallow

A bagged tree and a boosted tree. Which should be deep?

Answer. The bagged one. Averaging removes variance, so you want low bias going in. Boosting removes bias, so each term should be a small correction.
Warm-upW4 · The floor

Sixty trees, then six hundred. What stops improving?

Answer. The correlated part. Averaging drives the second term of §9's identity to zero and leaves the first untouched, so the floor is set by how much the trees agree.
Warm-upW5 · Two importances, two biases

Name the bias in impurity importance and in permutation importance.

Answer. Impurity favours features with many distinct values, because more thresholds means more chances at a lucky split. Permutation calls two correlated features unimportant, because permuting either leaves the signal in the other.
Warm-upW6 · What boosting fits

Under squared loss, what is the negative gradient?

Answer. The residual. Which is why the intuitive account (each tree fits what is left over) is exactly the gradient account, for that one loss.
Warm-upW7 · One hyperparameter or two

Learning rate and number of trees. How many decisions is that?

Answer. One. Halving the rate roughly doubles the trees needed, so fix the rate as low as the budget allows and early-stop the count on a validation set.
Warm-upW8 · Does boosting overfit

Answer from Figure 4.

Answer. Yes. At a learning rate of 1 the test error bottoms after 46 trees and rises after. The folk claim comes from AdaBoost's margin behaviour on separable classification and does not transfer.
Warm-upW9 · The encoder that leaks

Why is target encoding dangerous, and what is the repair?

Answer. The category's target mean is computed from rows including the row being encoded, so the feature carries the answer: exactly on a category with one row. Compute it out-of-fold or from preceding rows only.
Warm-upW10 · Which is calibrated

Boosting with logistic loss, or a random forest's vote fraction?

Answer. The boosting. It is minimising a proper scoring rule, so calibration is what it was fitted for. The vote fraction was never fitted to anything.
Warm-upW11 · Read Figure 7

Which family wins where, and what decides it?

Answer. Trees on thresholds and low-dimensional smooth terms; the network on linear targets and three-way products. The shape of the generating function decides it, not the data's format.
Warm-upW12 · The 18.8

What does a random split on a time series buy you?

Answer. A reported error 18.8 times better than the honest one, with every conventional diagnostic passing. The estimand was wrong, not the estimator.

Tier 2 Core

CoreC1 · Impurity, and the sum form

Implement sse and split_gain, and check they agree.

Answer. They must agree exactly. The sum form is what lets a single cumulative pass evaluate every threshold, and it is the reason a split search is not quadratic.
CoreC2 · Exact split search

Implement best_split. Plant a threshold and recover it.

Answer. Within a couple of hundredths. The subtlety is refusing to split between equal values: a threshold there separates identical inputs and cannot generalise.
CoreC3 · Grow a tree

Recurse on C2 with a depth limit. Reproduce Figure 1's two curves.

Answer. Training error to near zero, test error turning at a shallow depth. Count the leaves as you go: capacity here is not a proxy, it is the number itself.
CoreC4 · XOR at the root

Build a target that is the XOR of two features and try a depth-1 search.

Answer. It finds nothing: neither feature reduces impurity alone. A depth-2 tree represents it exactly. This is what greedy growth costs, and why an ensemble of shallow trees still captures interactions.
CoreC5 · Histogram splitting

Implement quantile_bins and histogram_best_split. Check it agrees with the exact search at fine binning.

Answer. It should land within a bin width. Then implement the sibling-subtraction trick (a child's histogram is its parent's minus its sibling's) and only build the smaller side.
CoreC6 · Sweep the bins

Reproduce Figure 5, averaging over several seeds.

Answer. Accuracy improves steeply to about 32 bins and then worsens. More thresholds means more chances to fit noise, so the coarse grid is regularising the split choice as well as saving time.
CoreC7 · The averaging identity

Implement averaging_variance and variance_floor. Confirm the limit.

Answer. The floor is rho times sigma squared and no number of trees reaches below it. Everything random forests do is an attempt on that first term.
CoreC8 · Out-of-bag

Implement oob_mask. Confirm the fraction, then build an out-of-bag error estimate.

Answer. About 1/e, and the estimate is free. It inherits the optimism of a random split whenever the rows are not exchangeable, so it is invalid on a series, for the same reason a random split is.
CoreC9 · Break permutation importance

Duplicate an informative column. Measure the importance of each copy.

Answer. Both come out near zero while the pair is essential. Then permute both together and watch the importance appear. Report groups, not columns, whenever columns are correlated.
CoreC10 · Gradient boosting, from the gradient

Implement negative_gradient, leaf_value and a stagewise loop.

Answer. Under squared loss the leaf value is the mean of the residuals in it. Then swap in the logistic loss and change nothing else: that substitutability is the point of deriving it as a gradient method.
CoreC11 · Second order

Implement structured_gain with the L2 and per-leaf penalties.

Answer. The same expression scores a split and sets the leaf, which is what makes the search and the fit one computation. Confirm a split of a homogeneous node scores negative once gamma is charged.
CoreC12 · Reproduce Figure 4

Sweep the learning rate with a fixed tree budget and plot staged test error.

Answer. The largest rate turns around; the smallest is still improving when the budget runs out. Report which curves converged: a 'best error' from an unconverged curve is a statement about the budget.
CoreC13 · Calibration, measured and repaired

Implement expected_calibration_error, isotonic_fit and auc. Reproduce Figure 6.

Answer. Boosting with logistic loss comes out best calibrated; the forest's vote fraction needs repair; isotonic moves the AUC by 0.0001. Then fit the calibrator on the training rows instead and watch the failure become invisible.
CoreC14 · Run the comparison properly

Reproduce Figure 7: four generating processes, both families tuned on the same grid, eight paired repetitions.

Answer. All four separate, two each way. Then run it once with a single split and see whether you would have believed the ratios: that is the argument for repetitions, made on your own data.
CoreC15 · Leak on purpose

Fit a scaler, an imputer and a target encoder on the full table before splitting. Measure the inflation.

Answer. Each one alone is worth something and together they are worth a great deal. Then move every one inside the fold and watch the honest number appear.
CoreC16 · Lags without the future

Implement lag_matrix for horizon 1 and horizon 5. Assert no row uses information at or after its target.

Answer. The assertion is the deliverable. Off-by-one errors here are silent, produce excellent validation scores, and are among the most common defects in deployed forecasting code.
CoreC17 · Reproduce Figure 8

Fit the same model on a series under a random split and a chronological one.

Answer. 18.8 times apart. Nothing is fitted incorrectly and no row appears twice. Then compare both against the last-value baseline, which is the number that says whether you have a model at all.
CoreC18 · Rolling origin

Implement rolling_origin_splits and assert every training index precedes every test index.

Answer. Then run expanding and sliding windows and compare. A large gap between them is evidence the process is changing, which is a diagnostic you get for free from the validation you were doing anyway.
CoreC19 · Beat seasonal naive

Implement seasonal_naive and try to beat it on a real seasonal series.

Answer. Harder than expected. Any forecasting result not reported against it should be read as unreported.
CoreC20 · Score a distribution

Implement pinball_loss and verify it is minimised at the intended quantile.

Answer. It is, at every tau you test. Then forecast three quantiles rather than a mean, and price a decision with them using Part XXIV §33's threshold.

Tier 3 Stretch

StretchS1 · A tree library, end to end

Assemble C2, C5 and C11 into a working gradient booster with early stopping and subsampling.

Answer. Roughly two hundred lines. Having written it, the hyperparameters stop being folklore and become things you can see acting.
StretchS2 · Leaf-wise against level-wise

Grow trees by always splitting the highest-gain leaf, and compare with level-wise at matched leaf counts.

Answer. Leaf-wise reaches a given training loss with fewer leaves and overfits sooner, which is why it needs its leaf count capped rather than its depth.
StretchS3 · Ordered target statistics

Implement the encoding of §20 with the out-of-fold repair, and measure the leak with and without.

Answer. The unrepaired version can look near-perfect in validation. The size of the gap depends on the cardinality of the column, which is worth measuring on your own worst offender.
StretchS4 · Missingness that means something

Build data where a field is blank precisely when the target is unusual. Compare learned default directions with imputation.

Answer. The tree finds it and the imputer destroys it. Then ask whether the pattern will survive to production, because that is what decides which behaviour you want.
StretchS5 · Ensemble the two families

Average a boosted model and a network on the same tabular problem, weighting on a validation split.

Answer. Usually better than either, and by more when their errors are less correlated: §9's identity, applied across families rather than within one.
StretchS6 · Reconcile a hierarchy

Forecast a three-level hierarchy independently, measure the incoherence, then reconcile.

Answer. The independent forecasts do not add up, and reconciliation typically improves accuracy at every level rather than only fixing the arithmetic.
StretchS7 · Coverage along the horizon

Reproduce Figure 9, then recalibrate on a rolling recent window and re-measure.

Answer. Recalibrating recovers some of the coverage and not all of it. Report the measured number rather than the nominal one, and if you widen the band to reach the promise, say that you did.
StretchS8 · Audit a tabular model you run

Take a deployed model and check the split, the encoders, the calibration and the baseline.

Answer. Most audits turn up a leak or an uncalibrated score. The one to check first is whether anything fitted on the full table crossed the fold boundary.
