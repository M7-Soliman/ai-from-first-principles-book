PracticeDrills

Thirty-three problems. The mechanism is short enough that you can implement all of it, and several of these ask you to verify a claim that is exact: the $\sqrt{d}$, RoPE's relative property, permutation equivariance, the identity FlashAttention preserves.

Tier 0 Estimate first

EstimateE1 · What attention costs in memory

Attention over a 4,096-token sequence. Write down how many entries the attention matrix has for one head, and how many megabytes that is at float32.

Answer. 16.8 million entries and 67 MB, per head, per layer. Multiply by 32 heads and 32 layers and the attention matrices alone exceed any single accelerator's memory, which is the entire reason the fused and flash formulations exist. Score yourself: within a factor of two is a pass, and an order of magnitude out is worth understanding before you go on.

Tier 1 Warm-up

WarmW1 · Three roles

Why does each position need both a key and a value?

Answer. The key answers "am I relevant to this query?" and the value answers "what should I hand over?". No reason one vector is good at both. Separating the address from the contents is what makes a lookup a lookup.
WarmW2 · The scale

Why $\sqrt{d_k}$ and not $d_k$, or $\log d_k$?

Answer. $\text{Var}[\mathbf{q}^\top\mathbf{k}] = d$ for unit-variance components, so the standard deviation is $\sqrt{d}$. Dividing by it returns the logits to unit variance at every width. It is the only scaling that does.
WarmW3 · Saturation

What goes wrong if the logits are large, and why is it fatal rather than merely untidy?

Answer. The softmax becomes an argmax. Its Jacobian $a_i(\delta_{ij} - a_j)$ is then near zero everywhere, so the layer stops learning, at initialisation, before it knows where to attend. It is frozen into a random choice.
WarmW4 · The symmetry

What symmetry does self-attention have, and why is it bad here when the analogous one was the point of Part X?

Answer. Permutation equivariance: exact, measured at $10^{-7}$. Bad because it is false of the task: dog bites man and man bites dog are the same multiset. Part X's translation symmetry was true of images.
WarmW5 · Path length

How many transformations separate the ends of a 16,384-token sequence, for attention, a recurrence, and a stack of $3\times3$ convolutions?

Answer. 1, 16,383, and 8,192. Constant against linear is what makes Part XI's Part C stop applying rather than merely improve.
WarmW6 · Where the parameters are

Split a block's parameters between attention and feedforward.

Answer. $4d^2$ against $8d^2$: 33% and 67%, exactly, at every width. Two thirds of the model is the part the architecture is not named after.
WarmW7 · The crossover

At what sequence length does attention overtake the feedforward layer in compute?

Answer. $n \approx 4d$. Below it, "attention is quadratic" is true and irrelevant: the feedforward layer dominates, and optimising attention buys little.
WarmW8 · Pre-norm

Write both placements, and say which one warmup was compensating for.

Answer. Post-norm: $\text{LN}(\mathbf{x} + f(\mathbf{x}))$. Pre-norm: $\mathbf{x} + f(\text{LN}(\mathbf{x}))$. Warmup was compensating for post-norm: the normalisation sits on the residual path and rescales the gradient at every block.
WarmW9 · The cache

Why does caching keys and values work, and what does it cost?

Answer. Earlier positions' keys and values do not change when a token is appended. Cost: memory linear in context and in concurrent requests, 68.7 GB at 131k tokens for a 32-layer, $d{=}4096$ model, several times the weights.
WarmW10 · Two layers

Why does an induction head need exactly two?

Answer. Layer 1 writes "what preceded me" into each position; layer 2 queries on that. The information layer 2 needs does not exist until layer 1 puts it there. A genuine composition.

Tier 2 Core

CoreC1 · Attention, from the formula

Implement scaled dot-product attention and check it against the library version, with and without a mask.

Answer. Agreement to float32 rounding. The mask must be applied before the softmax, as $-\infty$: masking after and renormalising is a different and wrong operation.
CoreC2 · Derive the scale

Measure $\text{Var}[\mathbf{q}^\top\mathbf{k}]$ against $d$, then measure the entropy of the resulting attention with and without the $\sqrt{d}$.

Answer. The variance ratio sits within about 10% of 1 across three decades of $d$. Unscaled entropy collapses from 2.70 to 0.10 against $\ln 64 = 4.16$; scaled, it holds near 3.7. Predicted, then measured, not fitted.
CoreC3 · Permutation equivariance

Verify $\text{SelfAttn}(PX) = P\,\text{SelfAttn}(X)$ numerically, then break it with a positional encoding and measure how much.

Answer. About $10^{-7}$ relative without positions, exact. With them, 0.5 to 1.1, which is the same order as the activations. The symmetry is destroyed, which is the intent.
CoreC4 · RoPE

Implement rotary embeddings and verify that $\mathbf{q}_m \cdot \mathbf{k}_n$ depends only on $m - n$. Test at absolute positions differing by thousands.

Answer. Agreement to about $10^{-12}$ in float64. Do it at positions 30 and 10,030; if you only test nearby positions you are not testing the property. The cancellation is $R_m^\top R_n = R_{n-m}$.
CoreC5 · Multi-head, and the parameter count

Implement multi-head attention. Confirm the parameter count does not depend on the number of heads.

Answer. Identical: $h$ projections of $d \times d/h$ is one of $d \times d$. Heads cost only reshaping. The usual bug is transposing before splitting rather than after, which silently mixes head and feature axes.
CoreC6 · The mask, verified properly

Do not inspect the mask; test its consequence. Change the token at position $j$ and confirm outputs at every position $< j$ are unchanged.

Answer. They must be bit-identical, not close. This is the one test that catches an off-by-one, and an off-by-one produces a beautiful training curve and a useless model.
CoreC7 · Time both halves

Time attention and the feedforward layer separately as $n$ grows, at fixed $d$. Find the crossover.

Answer. Near $n = 4d$. Below it attention is the cheaper half, which reframes the whole efficiency literature, and explains why methods with better asymptotics were often not adopted.
CoreC8 · The KV cache

Generate with and without a cache. Verify the outputs are identical, then measure the speedup and the memory.

Answer. Identical: it is an exact optimisation, not an approximation. Verify that first; a cache bug shows up as a small drift that is easy to mistake for sampling noise.
CoreC9 · Online softmax

Implement a streaming softmax that processes its input in tiles and matches the one-shot version exactly.

Answer. Keep a running maximum $m$ and sum $\ell$; on each tile rescale by $e^{m_{\rm old} - m_{\rm new}}$. Agreement to rounding. This is the entire mathematical content of FlashAttention: the rest is memory management.
CoreC10 · Rank collapse

Apply random attention layers repeatedly, with and without skip connections, and measure the effective rank of the representation.

Answer. Without skips it falls to 1.00: every position holds the same vector. With them it stays around 21. Attention outputs are convex combinations, and repeated averaging converges to a constant; $\mathbf{x} + f(\mathbf{x})$ is not a convex combination.
CoreC11 · Pre-norm against post-norm

Measure the gradient reaching the first block at initialisation, against depth, for both placements. Then train both at a high learning rate, with and without warmup.

Answer. Report all four runs. The claim to test is not "pre-norm is better" but "post-norm needs warmup and pre-norm does not", and the honest finding may be that at small depth neither fails, which is itself worth reporting.
CoreC12 · Linear attention

Implement $\phi(Q)(\phi(K)^\top V)$ and confirm the cost is linear in $n$. Then explain what you gave up.

Answer. $\phi(K)^\top V$ is $d \times d$: a fixed-size state, updated additively. You have rebuilt a recurrent network, and Part XI §28's bottleneck comes back with it. The quadratic cost and the unbounded state are the same fact.
CoreC13 · Grouped-query

Implement multi-query and grouped-query attention. Measure the cache size and the quality against full multi-head.

Answer. Cache divides by $h$ or $h/g$. Quality falls a little for multi-query and barely for grouped-query at $g \approx 8$, which is why the latter is the default. An architectural decision made by a deployment constraint.
CoreC14 · Attention is not explanation

Construct two attention distributions that differ substantially and produce near-identical outputs.

Answer. Easiest with values that nearly coincide, or by moving weight onto positions whose values are small. If several maps produce the same behaviour, no one of them is the explanation, which is why §36 calls them hypothesis-generating rather than evidence.

Tier 3 Stretch

StretchS1 · Find an induction head

Train a two-layer model on a repeated block at a random offset. Then locate the mechanism: which head in layer 1 is the previous-token head, and which in layer 2 does the matching?

Answer. The layer-1 head has attention concentrated on the sub-diagonal. The layer-2 head attends to "the token after the previous occurrence of the current token": measure it at about 3.6× uniform. Run the fixed-offset control too, or you have not shown the mechanism is needed.
StretchS2 · Ablate the circuit

Having found it, break it. Zero the layer-1 previous-token head and measure what happens to the layer-2 head's pattern and to the loss.

Answer. The layer-2 pattern should collapse: it was querying on information the first head supplied. This is the difference between correlational interpretability and a causal claim, and it is the standard by which Part XXI's work should be judged.
StretchS3 · In-context learning, measured properly

Train on many sequences each using a fresh random rule. At test time use rules never seen. Bucket accuracy by how many demonstrations of that specific query appeared earlier.

Answer. The conditioning is the point. Averaged over all positions the effect is diluted by queries the model cannot know: chance is correct there. Bucketed, the curve rises from chance with evidence, and no weight changed.
StretchS4 · Lost in the middle

Place a specific fact at a controlled depth in a long context and sweep the depth. Plot retrieval accuracy against position.

Answer. Expect better performance at the beginning and end than in the middle. The advertised context window is what the model will accept; this is what it uses, and the two differ.
StretchS5 · Extrapolate

Train with RoPE at a short context, then evaluate at four times the length. Then try interpolating the frequencies and re-evaluate.

Answer. Sharp degradation past the trained length: the model has never seen those rotation angles. Interpolation compresses the angles back into the trained range and recovers much of it, usually with a short fine-tune. This is where practical long-context work actually happens.
StretchS6 · Quantize, and find the outliers

Quantise a trained model's weights to 8 and 4 bits. Then look at the activations and find the outlier dimensions.

Answer. A few dimensions carry values orders of magnitude larger than the rest, reliably in the same places. Naive quantisation sets its range by them and collapses everything else. Keep those dimensions in higher precision and most of the loss goes away.
StretchS7 · A router

Implement a mixture-of-experts feedforward layer with top-$k$ routing. Measure the load balance across experts with and without an auxiliary balancing loss.

Answer. Without balancing, a few experts receive most tokens and the rest are dead: the router found a shortcut. Note also that top-$k$ is discrete and has no gradient; the standard fix weights outputs by the router's probabilities, which is a soft relaxation of the same kind attention itself is.
StretchS8 · Watch it break

Train a deep transformer at a learning rate high enough to produce loss spikes. Log the maximum attention logit and the per-head attention entropy alongside the loss.

Answer. The logit maximum grows before the loss spikes, and entropy collapses with it, so you have a leading indicator rather than a post-mortem. Then test the remedies: QK normalisation, a lower $\beta_2$, tighter clipping.
