BuildThe project

Ten to fourteen hours. You implement four generative models on one distribution you constructed, and then build the evaluation suite that can tell them apart, including the measurements that would have told you the wrong thing.

P.1 What you are building

gen: four models of one known distribution, and an evaluation suite with enough instruments to catch each family's characteristic failure.

The rule: choose the distribution before you choose the models, and choose one whose weights, modes and density you can write down. Every claim in this project is then checkable arithmetic rather than a judgement about pictures.

P.2 Stage 1: the distribution

  1. A mixture with unequal weights and at least six well-separated modes. Unequal, because equal weights make mode-dropping invisible in the aggregate; separated, because §7's mixing problem and §36's collapse both need room to appear.
  2. Write closed-form functions for the density, the score and the smoothed versions of both.
  3. Verify the density integrates to 1 and the score matches a finite difference.

Deliverable: the two verification numbers. Everything downstream compares against these, so an error here is an error everywhere.

P.3 Stage 2: the evaluation suite, first

Build this before any model, so you cannot tune the measurements to flatter the results.

  1. Recovered mixing weights and total variation from the truth.
  2. Modes covered, at a stated threshold.
  3. Precision and recall for distributions.
  4. MMD against a held-out real sample.
  5. Mean log-density of samples under the true density.
  6. Nearest-training-distance, for memorisation.

Deliverable: six functions, plus the value each returns for a fresh real sample. That row is your noise floor: Part XIV §19's discipline, and without it none of the model numbers can be read.

P.4 Stage 3: four models

  1. Autoregressive over discretised coordinates. Exact likelihood.
  2. VAE, reporting both the ELBO and an importance-weighted estimate.
  3. Diffusion, $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$-prediction across a cosine schedule.
  4. GAN, non-saturating, 1:1 updates.

Deliverable: one table: six metrics by four models, plus the real-sample row. Predict the table before you fill it in, and keep the prediction.

P.5 Stage 4: the measurements that mislead

  1. Construct a model that drops a mode and one that is correct plus broad noise. Run the full suite on both. Find the metrics that rank them in opposite orders.
  2. Sweep $\beta$ on the VAE and find a pair of models the ELBO ranks differently from the importance-weighted estimate.
  3. Build the model that samples uniformly from the training set, and find which of your six instruments catches it.

Deliverable: three concrete cases where a standard measurement says something untrue, from your own runs, with the number that would have misled you written down.

P.6 Stage 5: where collapse comes from

  1. Train the GAN under four conditions: standard, saturating loss, discriminator over-trained 5:1, and a high Adam $\beta_1$. Three seeds each.
  2. Report modes covered and total variation for all twelve runs.
  3. Then run diffusion on the identical data, and put it in the same table.

Deliverable: the twelve-run table, and a sentence on whether the loss form or the optimisation balance determined the outcome. If your result disagrees with §36's, say so and give your numbers; that is a finding, not a failure.

P.7 Stage 6: the score, verified

  1. Extract the implied score from your diffusion model at four noise levels.
  2. Compare it to the exact analytic score of the correspondingly smoothed density: cosine and magnitude ratio, separately.
  3. Then compare against the unsmoothed score and observe which of the two numbers breaks.

Deliverable: eight numbers, and the sentence explaining why the magnitude is the one that goes wrong.

P.8 Stretch

  • Guidance. Condition on the mixture component, sweep the weight and find the point past which mean log-density falls.
  • Flow matching on the same data, compared to diffusion at matched steps.
  • Langevin on the exact score, and the weights it fails to recover.
  • An energy-based model with contrastive divergence at $k \in \{1, 5, 100\}$.
  • Latent diffusion: autoencode first, then diffuse in the latent space.

P.9 Done when

  • Your density integrates to 1 and your score matches a finite difference.
  • The evaluation suite existed before the models did.
  • Every metric has a real-sample row, so you know its noise floor.
  • Four models are in one table, and you predicted it first.
  • You have one pair of models that two metrics rank in opposite orders.
  • You have one pair the ELBO ranks differently from the true likelihood.
  • You know which of your instruments catches a model that memorises.
  • You can say whether collapse in your runs came from the loss or the balance.
  • Your README names one metric whose value you predicted wrongly.
Why this project

Stage 2 is the reason it is ordered this way. Almost every published generative-model result chooses its metrics after seeing its samples, and the metrics in this area are loose enough that the choice matters. Building the suite first is the only version of this exercise that can surprise you.

Stage 4 is the second reason. This part's own figures produced three results that contradicted the standard account: the saturating loss was the best GAN condition, the ELBO ranked two models backwards, and guidance past its peak pushed samples off the distribution rather than concentrating them. All three were found by measuring something that is usually asserted. Expect at least one of yours to go the same way.

The one thing to carry forward

Every generative model is a different answer to the same question: what do we do about the normalising constant.

Autoregressive models make it a product of small ones and pay at sampling time. Latent variable models bound it and pay in a gap they cannot see. Implicit models refuse to represent it and lose the ability to score anything. Score-based models differentiate it away (which is why they won, because the difficulty was not relocated, it was removed).

And the second thing, because you will need it sooner: the two ways of being wrong are not symmetric, and no single number reports both. Report precision and recall, and say which failure you were looking for.
