Before you start

Parts 0 through II built the mathematics. This part is where the mathematics meets the machine, and the machine does not cooperate.

§0.1 What this part is about

Every derivation so far assumed exact arithmetic. Real numbers were real; sums were sums; if two expressions were algebraically equal, they were equal.

None of that survives contact with a computer. Numbers are stored approximately. Algebraically identical expressions produce different answers. Some produce no useful answer at all. And the difference is not a rounding detail in the last decimal place: it is routinely the difference between a training run that converges and one that returns nan after four hundred steps for reasons nobody can locate.

This part covers three connected things:

  • Precision: what a floating point number actually is, how it fails, and the small set of techniques that make code survive.
  • Conditioning: why some problems amplify error catastrophically no matter how good your algorithm is, and how to tell in advance.
  • Optimisation: the algorithms that actually minimise things, from gradient descent through curvature-aware methods to the stochastic and adaptive variants every modern system uses.
Why this matters

This is the most immediately practical part of the book. Almost everything in it will change what you do the next time you write code.

The failure modes here are the ones you will actually meet: a loss that becomes nan, a model that trains beautifully at one learning rate and diverges at 1.2 times that rate, an optimiser that crawls for ten thousand steps on a problem another optimiser solves in fifty, a linear solve that returns confident nonsense. Every one of those is a topic in this part, and every one has a diagnosis.

§0.2 What you need

  • Part 0: derivatives, the chain rule, gradients, and gradient descent. Section 26 introduced floating point; this part goes considerably deeper.
  • Part I: matrices, eigenvalues, symmetric matrices, the SVD. Section 13's discussion of conditioning is picked up and completed here.
  • Part II: expectation and variance, for the stochastic methods in §14 onward. Nothing else.

§0.3 The habit for this part

Parts 0 and I each had one: say what is random and what is fixed, and write down the shape. This part's is:

Never trust a number you have not sanity-checked.

Floating point failures are silent. There is no exception, no warning, no crash, and the computation returns a number, and the number is wrong. The only defence is to know in advance which operations are dangerous, and to check results against something independent: a known answer, a second algorithm, a simulation, or a bound you can derive by hand.

That habit is what §20 is built to demonstrate, and it is what the project asks you to practise.
