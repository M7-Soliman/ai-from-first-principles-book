BuildThe project

Ten to fourteen hours. You implement the bounds, measure them against the quantities they bound, and find the exact point at which they stop saying anything; then build the two things that still work when they have.

P.1 What you are building

bounded: a small library of generalisation bounds with a harness that evaluates each one against a measured gap, an exact VC-dimension calculator by enumeration, and an online learner with a regret tracker.

The rule: every bound gets evaluated as a number and compared with the thing it bounds. A bound quoted symbolically is a claim; a bound quoted numerically next to the measured gap is a result, and this part exists because the two look very different.

P.2 Stage 1: concentration

  1. Implement Hoeffding and Bernstein. Simulate the tail and confirm neither is violated.
  2. Sweep the variance and find where the two cross.
  3. Compute the sample size each demands for a target interval, and compare with the exact binomial answer.

Deliverable: the tail plot, and the ratio of distribution-free to exact sample size. That ratio is the price of a guarantee that holds for every distribution.

P.3 Stage 2: VC dimension, by enumeration

  1. Implement exact separability by linear programming.
  2. Enumerate all $2^m$ labellings and find where shattering stops, for linear classifiers in two and three dimensions.
  3. Do it again for axis-aligned rectangles.
  4. Implement Sauer's bound and check the dichotomy.

Deliverable: measured VC dimensions for two hypothesis classes, obtained without citing a theorem. The transition should be total, not gradual.

P.4 Stage 3: the bound against the gap

  1. Measure the real generalisation gap for a linear classifier across sample sizes, and plot the VC bound beside it.
  2. Evaluate the same bound at three realistic network sizes. Handle the regime where the dimension exceeds the sample without producing nan.
  3. Train a network on randomly labelled data and report the training error.

Deliverable: the ratio table, and a one-paragraph statement of why step three forecloses a distribution-free repair. This is the argument the part is built around.

P.5 Stage 4: past the threshold

  1. Build a random-feature regressor and sweep the feature count through $p = n$.
  2. Locate the peak, and confirm the second descent goes below the classical optimum.
  3. Add ridge regularization and sweep again.
  4. Construct several exact interpolants of one underdetermined problem and compare their norms and test errors.
  5. Run gradient descent from zero and from a large random start.

Deliverable: the double-descent curve with and without ridge, and the interpolant table. Step five is the one to think about: the implicit bias is toward the initialisation, and minimum-norm is the special case of starting at the origin.

P.6 Stage 5: no assumptions at all

  1. Implement regret, follow-the-leader and multiplicative weights.
  2. Construct the sequence that gives the leader linear regret, and confirm multiplicative weights is sublinear on it.
  3. Sweep the learning rate and find where it degenerates back into the leader.
  4. Convert to bandit feedback and measure the extra cost.

Deliverable: regret curves for both algorithms and both feedback models, with the $\sqrt{K}$ gap measured rather than quoted.

P.7 Stage 6: when the distribution moves

  1. Implement importance weighting and run the shift sweep with a well-specified and a misspecified model.
  2. Measure the variance the weights add when they are not needed.
  3. Implement label-shift correction by confusion-matrix inversion and recover a prior change you set.
  4. Build a spurious-correlation dataset. Train to minimise the average and then the worst group, and report both by group.

Deliverable: the two-panel shift result, and the group table. Note whether your average-minimising model is below chance on the conflicting group.

P.8 Stretch

  • A PAC-Bayes bound evaluated on a small trained network, with compression.
  • Stability of SGD: two datasets differing in one point, divergence against steps.
  • Sharpness measured, then defeated by layer rescaling.
  • Grokking on modular arithmetic with weight decay.
  • Rademacher complexity estimated for a real model by fitting random labels.
  • Your own worst group, found by searching partitions and confirmed out of sample.

P.9 Done when

  • You have simulated a tail and confirmed both bounds hold, and know the ratio.
  • You have measured a VC dimension for two hypothesis classes by enumeration.
  • You have a bound and a measured gap on the same axes, with the ratio stated.
  • You have evaluated a bound that comes out above 1, and can say why that is not fixable.
  • You have trained a model to zero error on random labels.
  • You have a double-descent curve, and the same curve with ridge.
  • You have four exact fits of one dataset with test errors spanning an order of magnitude.
  • You have defeated follow-the-leader and beaten it with multiplicative weights.
  • You have a covariate-shift correction that helps in one panel and does nothing in the other.
  • Your README states one guarantee you believed applied to your own models and now know does not.
Why this project

The bounds in this part are usually met as symbols and never evaluated. Evaluating them takes one line each and changes what you think they are for: a VC bound is not a loose guarantee about your network, it is not a guarantee about your network at all.

Stage 4 is the reason the part is here rather than in an appendix. The classical picture says capacity past the interpolation threshold is a mistake, and the measurement says the best model in the sweep is on the far side of it. Everything that has been proposed to explain that gap is a statement about the algorithm, and you can watch the algorithm making the choice.

The framework, and where the guarantee attaches

Dataset, model family, loss, optimiser, then Part XXI's claim about what the result establishes and Part XXII's estimand. This part says where a guarantee can attach to any of them, and the answer is narrower than the framework suggests.

It cannot attach to the model family, because the family provably admits no useful distribution-free bound at modern sizes. It attaches partially to the optimiser, and that is the live research direction. Mostly, in practice, it does not attach at all: what you have is a held-out set and Part XXI §27's arithmetic about how much that establishes. Knowing which of those you are relying on is the difference between a guarantee and a habit.
