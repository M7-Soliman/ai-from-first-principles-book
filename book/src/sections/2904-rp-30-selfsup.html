Part CSelf-supervision

Labels are expensive and raw data is not. This movement is about objectives that manufacture a supervised problem out of unlabelled data: the idea that failed in 2006, and the form of it that worked.

§15 The idea, and its first failure

Greedy layer-wise unsupervised pretraining

The procedure that restarted the field in 2006. Train a single-layer model (a restricted Boltzmann machine, or a shallow autoencoder) on the raw data. Freeze it, feed its output to a second, train that. Repeat. Then stack the layers, add a classifier, and fine-tune the whole thing with labels.

Before this, only convolutional networks and recurrent networks were considered trainable at depth. This was the first method that trained a deep fully connected network successfully, and it is the reason the 2006–2012 period happened at all.

By 2016 the chapter this part is built on could write that it "has been largely abandoned", having spent twenty pages on it. It is worth being precise about why, because the successor reuses more of it than the obituary suggests.

Why it was abandoned: four reasons, in order of weight

The problem it solved stopped existing. It was a fix for the difficulty of optimising deep networks. Rectifiers (Part VII §7), better initialisation (Part IX §16), batch normalisation (Part IX §23) and residual connections (Part X §23) solved that directly. A fix for a problem you no longer have is overhead.

Labels arrived in quantity. On datasets with thousands of labelled examples per class, supervised training with dropout beat it outright. Its advantage was always largest when labels were scarcest, and for a decade labels stopped being scarce in the benchmarks people used.

Two phases, and no dial. Every other regularizer has a coefficient you can turn. This one is on or off, with a second full set of hyperparameters whose effect cannot be assessed until the supervised phase finishes. Part XIV §17's complaint, in a different costume.

Greedy is greedy. Each layer is optimised in ignorance of the layers above it, so nothing coordinates them. The lower layers are never revisited in light of what the upper ones needed.

Pitfall: reading the obituary as a verdict on unsupervised learning

It was a verdict on greedy layer-wise pretraining as an optimisation aid, and it was correct. It was widely read as a verdict on learning representations without labels, and that reading was wrong within five years.

The exception was visible at the time and named in the source: language. Words as one-hot vectors carry no similarity information at all (every pair is equidistant) so the initial representation is as poor as it can be and there is enormous room for pretraining to help. Pretrained word vectors were standard practice throughout the period when pretraining was considered dead everywhere else.

The correct generalisation was available: unsupervised pretraining helps most when the starting representation is worst and unlabelled data is most abundant. That is a statement about a ratio, not about images or text, and the ratio moved.

§16 Pretext tasks

Manufacturing a label

A pretext task is a supervised problem whose labels are generated from the data itself. Rotate an image by a multiple of 90° and predict which. Cut it into tiles, shuffle them, predict the permutation. Remove the colour and predict it. Cut out a patch and inpaint it.

None of these is wanted for its own sake. The bet is that solving them requires knowing what objects look like, and that the representation built along the way is the useful part.

Why this generation of methods plateaued

They worked, and not well enough, and the reason is a mismatch that §19 makes precise.

A pretext task specifies what must be predicted. What you actually want to specify is what may be ignored. These are not the same, and the gap shows up as shortcuts: a network predicting rotation can read the sky's position rather than learning about objects; one solving jigsaws can match tile edges pixel by pixel. Part XIII §8's shortcut learning, arriving in the objective rather than the dataset.

Every pretext task ends up carrying a specific, accidental bias determined by which shortcut happened to be available. The next generation removed the intermediate task and stated the invariance directly.

§17 Contrastive learning

In 60 seconds

Take one example, make two altered views of it, and require their representations to agree. Require the representations of different examples to disagree. There is no reconstruction, no prediction and no label: only a statement about which pairs of things should end up near each other.

InfoNCE

For a batch of $N$ examples, form two views of each. Let $\mathbf{z}_i$ and $\mathbf{z}_i^{+}$ be the normalised representations of the two views of example $i$. Then

$$\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \log \frac{\exp\!\big(\mathbf{z}_i \cdot \mathbf{z}_i^{+} / \tau\big)} {\sum_{j=1}^{N} \exp\!\big(\mathbf{z}_i \cdot \mathbf{z}_j^{+} / \tau\big)}$$

This is softmax cross-entropy over the batch, where the correct class is "the other view of me", Part II §25's function with an unusual choice of what counts as a class. The temperature $\tau$ controls how sharply near misses are penalised; small $\tau$ concentrates the gradient on the hardest negatives.

Where the negatives come from

SimCLR uses the other examples in the batch, which makes the batch size the number of negatives and pushes it into the thousands.

MoCo decouples the two with a queue of representations from recent batches, encoded by a slowly-updated momentum copy of the encoder. This buys many negatives at a small batch size; the momentum encoder exists because representations from an encoder that has since moved are not comparable to current ones.

§18 What the negatives are for

Why the objective needs a term that pushes outward

Consider the objective with the denominator removed: maximise agreement between views and nothing else. It has an exact, trivial, global optimum: map every input to the same vector. Agreement is perfect. The representation contains nothing.

This is not a hypothetical failure mode reachable through bad luck. It is the minimum, and gradient descent finds it.

The denominator is what prevents it. Pushing different examples apart is a force for uniformity on the sphere, and the numerator is a force for alignment of matched pairs. The two together are the entire mechanism, and they are the same compromise as §14's two forces with the first one restated.

§19 The augmentation is the specification

Go and read

Chen et al. (2020), A simple framework for contrastive learning of visual representations. SimCLR. Skip to the augmentation ablation, which is this section's figure run on real images: the composition of augmentations is the design decision, and no single one suffices. Then Wang & Isola (2020), which decomposes the objective into alignment and uniformity and shows both can be optimised directly.

The most consequential design decision in contrastive learning is not the loss, the temperature or the architecture. It is the choice of how the two views may differ.

Why the augmentation is a specification and not a trick

Augmentation is usually introduced as a way to get more data out of what you have. In a contrastive objective it is doing something categorically different, and the figure is the demonstration.

Saying "these two views are the same thing" says the factor I varied does not matter. The objective then does exactly that and nothing more: the varied factor is discarded, the rest is kept. Colour jitter says colour is not identity. Cropping says position and scale are not identity. There is no other channel through which the objective learns what to keep.

So the augmentation set is the definition of the task, written in a language of invariances instead of labels. This is what §16's pretext tasks could not express directly, and it is why the generation that replaced them worked.

Pitfall: the invariance you declared is the invariance you get

The middle bars of the figure are the warning. Augment away the factor you needed and it is gone: class decodability falls to 0.193, barely above chance, while the nuisance is retained at 0.999. The objective did precisely what it was told.

Colour jitter is standard, and it destroys a representation for bird species identification, where plumage colour is the signal. Aggressive cropping destroys counting and spatial reasoning. Rotation invariance is right for aerial imagery and wrong for handwritten digits, where it merges 6 with 9.

There is no general-purpose augmentation set, and a pretrained encoder inherits the invariances of whoever trained it, usually undocumented. When a published encoder underperforms on your task, the first question is which of your signal it was trained to ignore.

Note also the third pair of bars: with no augmentation the objective keeps everything (0.965 and 0.963, effective rank 31 of 32). It has not been asked to discard anything, so it does not. Invariance is never free and never automatic.
