Part FEvaluation

The source book ends its generative chapter by saying that designing ways to measure progress is as important a research problem as improving the models. A decade later that is still the most accurate sentence available.

§40 Why this is unsolved

What makes it structurally harder than classification

A classifier has a right answer per example, so accuracy is well defined and Part XIV's whole apparatus applies. A generative model has no right answer for any individual sample: the question is about a distribution, and you have samples from it rather than the thing itself.

Three consequences compound. The quantity you want, a divergence between two distributions, cannot be estimated well in high dimensions from samples. The quantity you can sometimes compute, a likelihood, is not the one anyone cares about (§41). And the quantity people actually use, how the samples look, is insensitive to the most important failure (§42).

§41 Likelihood, and what it fails to measure

Go and read

Theis, van den Oord & Bethge (2016), A note on the evaluation of generative models. Short, and the most useful thing written on the subject. Establishes that log-likelihood, sample quality and nearest-neighbour checks are largely independent: a model can excel at any one while failing the others. Everything in this movement is downstream of it.

Pitfall: four ways a likelihood misleads

It is dominated by the easy parts. In a real-valued model, likelihood can be driven arbitrarily high by assigning very low variance to components that barely vary: background pixels, silence, padding. A model that does nothing but nail the trivial dimensions outscores one that captures the interesting ones.

Mode dropping is punished far more than garbage. Forward KL is infinite where the data has mass and the model has none, and merely finite where the model has spurious mass. The figure is this asymmetry, measured.

Preprocessing changes it. Multiplying inputs by 0.1 raises the density by a factor of $10^n$. Binarising an image dataset one way rather than another changes the number completely. Two likelihoods are comparable only under identical preprocessing, which for a while meant researchers shared the exact binarised file.

Most of the reported numbers are not likelihoods. They are bounds (§17), or importance estimates, or annealed-importance estimates whose error is unknown and one-sided. A bound and an estimate cannot be compared without knowing the slack, and §17 measured a case where the bound gets the ordering backwards.

§42 Sample quality, and what it fails to measure

Why looking at samples cannot detect the important failure

The figure's middle model produces flawless samples and has dropped a third of the distribution. §36's collapsed GAN produced 1 mode of 8, and every sample it produced was a genuine mode.

The reason inspection fails is arithmetic. To notice that a mode is missing you must remember what should be there and observe its absence across many samples. A human inspecting a grid of images is well equipped to spot an implausible sample and badly equipped to spot an absent category, and the difficulty grows with the number of modes. On data with thousands of modes, dropping dozens is undetectable by eye.

This is why the field's most-used evaluation, look at some samples, is systematically blind to the failure that most distinguishes the model families.

The nearest-neighbour check, and why it is not optional

The opposite failure is memorisation: a model that reproduces training examples has perfect sample quality and has learned nothing you wanted.

The minimum check is to show, for a sample, its nearest neighbours in the training set, by a distance in a space where distance means something. It is weak, near-duplicates that are not pixel-near will pass, and it is far better than nothing, and it is frequently omitted.

Part XIII §33's memorisation and Part XIII §15's deduplication both bear on this: a corpus with duplicates produces a model that reproduces them, and the check will not flag it because the duplicate is genuinely a distinct training row.

§43 Automatic sample metrics

Go and read

Kynkäänniemi et al. (2019), Improved precision and recall metric for assessing generative models. The two-number replacement for a single distance, with a support estimate that works from samples. What should be reported by default and usually is not.

FID, and what it actually computes

Embed real and generated images with a fixed pretrained network, fit a Gaussian to each set of embeddings, and report the Fréchet distance between the two Gaussians.

It correlates with human judgement better than anything before it and it is the field's default. Its assumptions should be stated at least once: that the embedding preserves what matters, and that two Gaussians summarise the distributions, a strong claim about data whose whole interest is that it is multimodal.

Pitfall: FID's known defects

It is biased by sample count, strongly, and the bias does not vanish at practical sizes. FID at 10,000 samples and at 50,000 are different numbers for the same model, so the count must be reported and matched.

It depends on the embedding network, including its exact weights and preprocessing. Numbers from different implementations are not comparable and have been compared anyway.

It is a single number over two failure modes. Producing too little variety and producing wrong things both raise it, and it cannot say which: which is exactly the distinction §41 and §42 established as the one that matters.

That last defect has a repair, and it should be the default report.

Precision and recall, for distributions

Part XIV §3's pair, applied to samples rather than predictions. Estimate the support of each distribution from its samples, then:

Precision is the fraction of generated samples that fall in the data's support: are the things you made real? Recall is the fraction of real samples that fall in the model's support: did you make everything?

A mode-dropping model has high precision and low recall. A model producing garbage has the reverse. The two numbers separate exactly what FID conflates, and the figure's three models would be immediately distinguished by them where a single distance would not.

The general lesson is Part XIV §3's: a single number over a two-sided failure cannot be read. It took this field about as long to accept that as it took classification.

§44 What to report

A minimum honest report
  • Which quantity the likelihood number is (exact, bound or estimate) and for a bound or estimate, how it was computed and with what $k$.
  • Preprocessing, exactly, since it changes the number.
  • Sample-based metrics with the sample count, and the embedding network's identity.
  • Precision and recall separately, not only a single distance.
  • A memorisation check, with the space the distance was computed in.
  • The sampler: number of steps, solver and guidance weight. §30 showed that guidance changes the distribution being sampled, so a sample metric at $w=3$ is not a measurement of the model.
  • Seeds, per Part XIV §19, and the search size, per Part XIV §20.

§46 What to carry forward

The one thing

Every generative model is a different answer to the same question: what do we do about the normalising constant.

Autoregressive models make it a product of small ones and pay at sampling time. Latent variable models bound it and pay in a gap they cannot see. Implicit models refuse to represent it and lose the ability to score anything, including themselves. Score-based models differentiate it away, and that is why they won, because the difficulty was not moved somewhere else, it was removed.

The second thing, which is the part most likely to be needed: the two ways of being wrong are not symmetric, and no single number reports both. A model can produce only real things and omit most of them, or produce everything and much besides. Likelihood punishes the first far harder; looking at samples cannot see it at all. Report precision and recall, and say which of the two failures you were checking for.

Four results worth remembering as numbers
  • Denoising recovers the distribution, not just its support. Mixing weights recovered to total variation 0.0075, below the 0.0093 of a real draw of the same size.
  • The ELBO can rank two models backwards. It preferred $\beta=0.2$; the true likelihood preferred $\beta=4$.
  • Mode collapse is about the balance, not the loss. The saturating objective covered 8/8 modes; a 5:1 discriminator covered 1.
  • Guidance past its peak leaves the distribution. Log-density $-1.78$ at $w=2$, $-165.5$ at $w=8$, with the spread growing rather than shrinking.
