Preface

Artificial intelligence is not a mystery. It is a small number of ideas, applied with enormous force, and every one of them can be understood by anyone willing to work through the arithmetic.

§ The situation

We have built systems that recognise faces, translate between languages, diagnose from medical images, write code, and hold conversations. They are deployed in hospitals, courtrooms, banks and classrooms. Their behaviour affects people who never consented to it and mostly cannot inspect it.

And the number of people who genuinely understand how they work (not at the level of calling an API, but at the level of being able to derive why a model does what it does) is far smaller than it should be.

That gap is not caused by the difficulty of the material. It is caused by how the material is taught.

§ Two failure modes

The literature splits into two kinds of text, and each fails a different reader.

The tutorial shows you which library functions to call. You can build something that works, and you learn nothing about why. When it stops working (and it will, because these systems fail in ways that look like nothing at all) you have no purchase on the problem. You are left adjusting numbers and hoping.

The reference states everything correctly and compresses ruthlessly. One paragraph carries a semester of implied background. It is written for the reader who already knows, and it is invaluable to that reader. For anyone else it is a wall: you read thirty dense pages, understand sixty percent, tell yourself the rest will come later, and discover four chapters on that it did not come later; it compounded.

Neither failure is anyone's fault. Tutorials are honest about being introductions. References are honest about being references. What is missing is the thing in between: a treatment that assumes nothing, derives everything, and does not stop until the reader can do the work themselves.

§ What this book is

A complete, self-contained treatment of artificial intelligence, built from first principles.

It begins with arithmetic. Not as a gesture: literally. Part 0 rebuilds the mathematics from algebra through gradient descent, and ends by working a full backpropagation step by hand, with real numbers you can check on paper. From there the book builds linear algebra, probability and information theory, numerical computation, the theory of learning from data, and then the architectures themselves.

The governing rule is simple and absolute: nothing is assumed that has not already been established. Every prerequisite is taught here, in order, before it is needed. There are no silent leaps, no "it can be shown that", no appeals to background the reader was supposed to acquire elsewhere.

Where a result matters, it is derived. Where a definition looks arbitrary, the book explains what question it answers. Where a step usually takes a page to justify, the page is written. There are worked examples with actual numbers throughout, because a formula you have never evaluated is a formula you do not own.

§ Who it is for

Anyone willing to do the exercises.

Concretely: a student who wants the foundations rather than the recipes. A working engineer who has been shipping models and would like to know what they are. A researcher from another field who needs this one and does not have four years. Someone whose mathematics has gone quiet from disuse: being rusty is not being unable, and rust comes off faster than most people expect.

You do not need prior machine learning, statistics, or linear algebra. You do not need a graduate degree, a GPU, or permission. You need arithmetic, some patience, and a willingness to compute things rather than read about them.

§ Three commitments

Every figure is computed. Not one is sketched, stylised or approximated. When a figure shows that certain directions are unchanged by a transformation, the code computed those directions and drew what it found. When a figure shows an optimiser diverging, those are real iterations at a real step size. If a picture would have to lie in order to look tidy, the picture is wrong and gets rebuilt.

Difficulty is stated honestly. Some of this material is hard. There are two or three places where careful readers slow down, and slowing down there is correct rather than a warning sign. Where something is genuinely difficult, this book says so. Where a result is easier than its reputation, it says that too.

Understanding is verifiable. Every part ends with drills backed by an automated test harness and a project substantial enough to expose whether you actually understood. Green tests mean you are right. Feeling right means nothing, and the difference between those two is the entire reason the exercises exist.

§ On history

There is a great deal of history in these pages, more than is fashionable, and it is there on purpose.

Mathematics is usually taught as though it arrived finished, a set of definitions that were always obviously correct, handed down from nowhere. It did not arrive that way. Logarithms were invented to spare astronomers years of arithmetic. Least squares was invented to find a lost asteroid. Probability began as a dispute over how to divide a pot when a game was interrupted. The decomposition at the heart of modern data analysis was discovered independently at least five times, which is what happens when an idea is genuinely natural rather than merely clever. Entropy got its name partly because of a joke, and the joke tells you something true about the concept.

Knowing this changes how the material feels. A definition that looks arbitrary almost always turns out to be the answer to a specific question somebody urgently needed answered, and the question is frequently more memorable than the answer. It also supplies a corrective that this field badly needs: the people who built these ideas were working without the benefit of knowing they would work, and most of them were wrong for a while first.

§ What comes after the mathematics

The foundations are not the destination. They are what makes the destination legible.

Once the mathematics is in place, this book builds the systems: how a network is trained and why it generalises; what convolution buys and what it costs; how sequences are modelled and why attention displaced recurrence; how representations are learned without labels; how generative models sample; and how any of it is made to work at scale.

It also takes seriously the questions that follow from the machinery: what these systems can and cannot do, where they fail, how those failures are detected, and what follows from deploying them on people. Those questions are not a coda. They are engineering questions, and they have technical answers that depend on understanding the mechanism.

§ A closing note

If parts of this take longer than you expect, that is the material being hard, not you being slow. Some of these ideas took mathematicians centuries to work out properly. Spending a week on one of them is roughly the correct amount of time.

The people who find this subject tractable are not the ones who found it easy. They are the ones who did the exercises.

Soliman M.H.

How to read this book

§ In order, and slowly

The parts are sequential. Each assumes everything before it and nothing after it, which is unusual for a technical book and is the direct consequence of the governing rule. If you find yourself lost, the cause is almost always something earlier that was skimmed rather than something present that is too hard.

If your mathematics is solid, Part 0 can be skimmed, but take its checkpoint first rather than assuming. If it is not solid, Part 0 is the difference between this book working and not working.

§ Do not read passively

Roughly every third page asks you to compute something. The asking is the point. This material is a motor skill before it is a body of knowledge, and the gap between "I followed that" and "I can do that" is exactly where understanding fails.

Each part ends with drills in three tiers and a project. If you are short on time, skip the prose and do the drills. Recognition comes from reading; fluency comes only from working problems; and fluency is what you actually need.

§ The markers

In 60 seconds

Opens the load-bearing sections. The whole idea compressed to a few sentences, for deciding how carefully to read, and for returning months later.

Intuition

The picture in your head. If the formalism stops making sense, fall back here. Nothing in these boxes is a proof; each is the thing a proof is trying to make precise.

History

Where an idea came from and what its inventor was actually trying to do. Frequently the reason a definition takes the odd shape it does.

Watch the shapes

Dimension bookkeeping. About half of all real errors in this field are shape errors, and nearly all of them are preventable by writing the dimensions down.

Pitfall

A specific mistake that is easy to make and hard to see, usually somewhere ordinary intuition is actively misleading.

Why this matters

Where the idea reappears later and what breaks without it. If you wonder why you are being made to learn something, look for one of these.

Try it

Stop reading. Compute. Answers follow immediately, so there is no risk of getting stranded, but write yours down first. Reading an answer feels like understanding and is not.

Margin notes carry asides and cross-references that did not warrant a full box.
