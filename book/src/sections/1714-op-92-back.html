BuildThe project

Eight to twelve hours. You build the instruments this part kept asking for, and then use them to diagnose four deliberately broken training runs without looking at the answers.

P.1 What you are building

optiscope, a training harness that records, every epoch, the quantities §29's ladder needs, plus a set of sabotaged configurations to test it on.

The rule: every diagnosis must be made from the recorded measurements before the configuration is revealed. A diagnostic that only works once you know the answer is not a diagnostic.

P.2 Stage 1: the instruments

A training loop for a small network that logs, per epoch:

  • Training and validation loss, and the corresponding 0–1 error, both per §3.
  • Full-batch $\|\mathbf{g}\|$ and the mean minibatch $\|\hat{\mathbf{g}}\|$, separately. They tell you different things and Figure 3 plots both.
  • $\mathbf{g}^\top H\mathbf{g}$ by finite differences, and the implied ceiling $2\mathbf{g}^\top\mathbf{g}/\mathbf{g}^\top H\mathbf{g}$ alongside your actual rate.
  • Per-layer activation and gradient standard deviation.
  • Fraction of dead units, and $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|$.

Deliverable: reproduce Figure 3, a run whose validation error falls to a few percent while the gradient norm rises. Report the ceiling at the first and last epoch and the factor between them.

P.3 Stage 2: four sabotages, diagnosed blind

Write a second script that emits four broken configurations in a random order, each recording only what Stage 1 records. Have someone else run it, or write the diagnosis before you look.

  1. Initialisation scale wrong by a factor of ten, in a deep stack.
  2. Learning rate above the ceiling from about a third of the way through.
  3. Output bias at zero on a 99/1 imbalanced problem.
  4. Four regularisers at once, each individually defensible.

Deliverable: for each, the rung of §29's ladder that caught it and the specific number that gave it away. Note honestly any you got wrong: the loss curves are similar, which is the point of the exercise.

P.4 Stage 3: the equivalences

Three measurements that each connect two things this part treated separately.

  • Damping is ridge. Solve a quadratic with $[H+\alpha I]^{-1}\mathbf{g}$ at several $\alpha$ and confirm the per-eigendirection shrinkage is Part VIII §4's $\lambda/(\lambda+\alpha)$, the same operator, two chapters apart. Show the limit $\alpha \to \infty$ becoming gradient descent at rate $1/\alpha$.
  • The one recurrence, four times. Implement momentum, RMSProp's accumulator, batch normalisation's running statistics and Polyak's EMA, and confirm all four are $x \leftarrow \rho x + (1-\rho)u$ with different $u$. State each one's effective window.
  • Batch size and the Newton step. Reproduce Figure 2's right panel and report the amplification factor at your condition number.

P.5 Stage 4: normalisation, honestly

  1. Build a deep network that fails to train from PyTorch's default initialisation.
  2. Fix it with He initialisation alone. Record the range of workable learning rates.
  3. Add batch normalisation on top of the correct initialisation and record it again.
  4. Report the best loss reached by each.

Deliverable: the widening factor, and an honest statement of whether normalisation lowered the best loss or only widened the range. Comparing normalisation against a bad initialisation is the standard way this measurement is overstated, and step 2 exists to prevent it.

P.6 Stage 5: where the algorithm cannot help

Construct a problem with the structure of §12: a starting region not connected to the good solution by a path local descent will follow.

  1. Confirm that SGD, momentum, Adam and L-BFGS all fail from that start.
  2. Confirm that at least one of a different initialisation, a continuation schedule, or a stochastic curriculum succeeds.

Deliverable: the table of four failures and one success, and a paragraph on why this is the difficulty that "just use a better optimiser" cannot address.

P.7 Stretch

  • Saddle-free descent. On a small problem where you can afford the Hessian, replace $H^{-1}$ with $|H|^{-1}$ (absolute eigenvalues) and compare against plain Newton near a saddle.
  • The first epoch is special. Train on a stream with no repetition and confirm that training and validation loss stay together, then switch to repeated epochs and watch them separate. §5's claim, made visible.
  • Curriculum, both kinds. Deterministic and stochastic, against uniform sampling. Report the negative result too.
  • Auxiliary heads. Attach an extra output to a middle layer of a deep network, train, then discard it. Measure the gradient reaching layer one with and without.

P.8 Done when

  • You have a run where the gradient norm rises and validation error falls.
  • The step ceiling is logged alongside the learning rate, every epoch.
  • All four sabotages were diagnosed from measurements, with the misses recorded.
  • The damping–ridge equivalence is verified numerically in the eigenbasis.
  • All four uses of the exponential recurrence are implemented and their windows stated.
  • The normalisation comparison uses a correct initialisation in both arms.
  • Four optimisers fail on the same problem and a non-algorithmic remedy succeeds.
  • Your README names one thing you had been blaming on the optimiser that was not.
Why this project

Optimisation is where the gap between what practitioners can explain and what they can diagnose is widest. Almost everyone can state what Adam does. Very few can say, of a run in front of them, whether the problem is the initialisation, the curvature, the regularisation total or the architecture, and the reason is that they have never had the instruments.

Stage 2 is the part that changes how you work. Four failures that produce similar loss curves, distinguished by measurements that cost almost nothing, is an experience that does not survive being read about.

The one thing to carry forward

The optimiser is not solving your problem, and that is the arrangement rather than a defect.

It minimises a surrogate, on a sample, from a starting point you chose, until a criterion it cannot see tells it to stop. Every difficulty here follows from that, and so does every remedy, which is why the four things around the optimiser matter more than which optimiser you picked.

And when a run fails, the question is never "which optimiser should I try?" It is "what have I measured?"
