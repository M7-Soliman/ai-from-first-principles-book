§20 Collapse, measured

Effective rank

Collapse needs a measurement, and accuracy is not one. Take the covariance of the representation, normalise its eigenvalues to sum to one, and report

$$\exp\!\Big(-\sum_i p_i \log p_i\Big), \qquad p_i = \lambda_i \big/ \textstyle\sum_j \lambda_j$$

the exponential of the entropy of the spectrum. Isotropic data in $d$ dimensions returns approximately $d$. Data on a line returns 1, whatever the ambient dimension. It costs one SVD and it is the instrument to log during any self-supervised run.

Pitfall: a probe is not a collapse monitor

In this figure the two instruments agree, and that agreement is a property of the task rather than a general fact.

Built on an easier version of the same data, where the class is linearly readable straight off the raw input, the same positives-only run reaches effective rank 1.04 while a linear probe still reports 0.999, and the probe does not approach chance for another order of magnitude. The representation is one-dimensional and the probe calls it perfect, because the tiny surviving directions are enough when the property was nearly free to begin with.

Whether the probe notices also depends on the probe's own regularization: a penalty large enough to forbid the large weights needed to amplify a small direction will hide the signal that a weaker penalty finds. That is a hyperparameter of the instrument, and it is not usually reported.

So log the effective rank. It depends on neither the task's difficulty nor the probe's settings, and it is cheaper than the probe.

§21 Without negatives

Go and read

Chen & He (2021), Exploring simple Siamese representation learning. SimSiam, which strips BYOL to its essentials and finds the momentum encoder is not one of them. The ablations are the evidence that the predictor and the stop-gradient are each load-bearing, and the hypothesis section is unusually honest that the mechanism remains incompletely explained.

§18 argued that something must push outward or the representation collapses. Two methods appear to violate this: they use positive pairs only, no negatives, no term separating anything, and they do not collapse.

BYOL and SimSiam

Two views, two encoders. One branch has an extra predictor head; the other branch is the target, and the gradient does not flow through it, a stop-gradient. The loss asks the predictor's output to match the target's, and nothing else.

BYOL additionally makes the target an exponential moving average of the online encoder. SimSiam showed the moving average is not necessary: the same encoder on both sides works, provided the predictor and the stop-gradient are present.

Both components are load-bearing. Remove the predictor and it collapses. Remove the stop-gradient and it collapses. The figure's third curve is this arrangement holding effective rank 5.72 and probe accuracy 0.998, with no repulsive term anywhere in the objective.

Why the asymmetry substitutes for negatives

The collapsed solution is still a global optimum of the loss. What changes is whether gradient descent goes there.

The clearest account: the predictor learns to approximate the average target over the augmentations of an input, and the stop-gradient means the target is not simultaneously moving to meet it. The online branch chases a fixed point that is one optimisation step behind, and the dynamics of that chase do not pass through the constant solution. Collapse remains optimal and becomes unreachable.

This is a genuinely different kind of argument from the rest of this part, and worth flagging as such. Everywhere else, a property of the representation followed from a property of the objective. Here it follows from a property of the optimiser's trajectory. Part IX §4's observation that training halts somewhere that is not a critical point, turned into a design technique.

It is also less robust for that reason. An objective-level guarantee holds regardless of schedule; a dynamics-level one can be broken by a learning rate, and these methods have a reputation for sensitivity that follows directly.

§22 Masked modelling

Go and read

He et al. (2022), Masked autoencoders are scalable vision learners. MAE. Read §4's ablation on the mask ratio, which is where the 75% comes from: it is a measurement of how redundant images are, not a tuning result, and it is why the same objective needs a different ratio for text.

Remove part of the input and predict it

Hide a fraction of the input and train the model to reconstruct what was hidden, from what remains. There are no views, no negatives and no augmentation policy: the corruption is the objective.

This is §11's denoising autoencoder with masking as the corruption process, at scale and with a transformer. BERT masks about 15% of tokens and predicts them. MAE masks 75% of image patches, encodes only the visible ones, and reconstructs pixels with a light decoder.

Why the two mask ratios differ by a factor of five

The gap is not a tuning accident; it measures the redundancy of the two signals.

Language is dense. A token is largely unpredictable from its neighbours, so removing 15% already leaves a hard problem, and removing 75% would leave too little context to recover anything.

Images are extremely redundant. A patch is well predicted by the patches around it, so masking 15% gives a task solvable by local interpolation: texture continuation, learning nothing about objects. Only when most of the image is gone does the model have to represent what is in the picture in order to fill it in.

The general principle, and it transfers: the corruption must destroy information that local structure cannot restore. The right ratio is a property of the data's redundancy, and it is measurable: sweep it, and watch where the reconstruction stops being solvable by interpolation.

The efficiency consequence is large. MAE's encoder sees only the unmasked 25% of patches, so each step costs a fraction of a full forward pass, a case where the objective that works is also the cheap one.

Masked against contrastive, as a choice

Masked objectives need no augmentation policy, so nothing has to be specified about invariances and the failure of §19 cannot happen. They tend to produce representations that reward fine-tuning and probe less well linearly, because nothing required the features to be linearly separable.

Contrastive objectives produce strong linear probes, because the objective explicitly arranges the space by similarity, and they inherit exactly the invariances of the augmentation set, for better and worse.

A defensible default: masked when you will fine-tune and cannot state your invariances, contrastive when you need a frozen encoder whose output a linear model will consume. The two are routinely combined.

§23 What self-supervision buys

Why the benefit lives at the left-hand end

The two curves converge. Given enough labels, supervised training learns the structure itself and pretraining stops paying, which is exactly what happened to §15's greedy pretraining when benchmarks with thousands of labels per class became the standard.

What pretraining supplies is structure the labels would otherwise have to pay for. The unlabelled data reveals the shape of the input distribution; the labels then only have to name the regions. When labels are scarce, that division of work is decisive; when they are abundant, it is redundant.

So the quantity that predicts whether self-supervision helps is the ratio of unlabelled to labelled data, and the difficulty of the structure. That is the correct generalisation of §15's history: the method did not become right, the ratio moved.

Pitfall: pretraining is not free and the comparison is often unfair

The figure's left-hand end is a real effect and it omits the cost. The pretraining run saw far more data than the supervised arm and took far longer. Compared at equal total compute rather than at equal labels, the picture changes and the honest claim narrows.

The claim that survives: pretraining converts compute and unlabelled data into a reduced need for labels. That is a good trade when labels are the expensive resource, which is usually, and it is not a free improvement. Any comparison that reports label efficiency without reporting the pretraining budget has priced only one side.
