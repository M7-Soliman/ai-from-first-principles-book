Part FTransport, and practice

One more way to compare distributions (the one that works when the others return infinity), and then how to choose among everything this part has offered.

§40 Comparing distributions

Three families, and what each needs

Divergences by density ratio. KL and the rest of the $f$-family compare $p(x)$ against $q(x)$ pointwise. They need the two to overlap, and they say nothing about how far apart the non-overlapping parts are.

Integral probability metrics. Take the largest difference in expectation over a class of test functions. Maximum mean discrepancy is this with a kernel ball; a discriminator's objective is this with a neural class.

Transport distances. The cost of moving one distribution onto the other. These use the geometry of the space, which the first family ignores entirely, and that is the whole difference.

§41 Optimal transport

Wasserstein

$$W_p(\mu,\nu) \;=\; \left(\inf_{\gamma \in \Pi(\mu,\nu)} \int \lVert x - y \rVert^p \, d\gamma(x,y)\right)^{1/p}$$

The infimum is over couplings (joint distributions with the right marginals), so it is the cheapest plan for moving mass from one arrangement to the other. In one dimension it has a closed form: sort both samples and average the differences.

The general problem is a linear program and expensive; entropic regularisation adds a penalty on the coupling's entropy, which makes it solvable by alternately rescaling rows and columns of a matrix (Sinkhorn iteration) in a form that is differentiable and runs on a GPU. That practicality is why transport moved from a corner of analysis into machine learning.

Where this bites in practice

A generative model early in training puts its mass somewhere the data is not. Under a likelihood-ratio objective that is an infinite loss with a useless gradient, which is a large part of why GAN training was unstable and why Wasserstein formulations helped.

It is also why transport appears inside diffusion. Flow matching picks the path along which a sample travels from noise to data, and choosing the transport-optimal path makes those paths straight, which is exactly what lets a solver take fewer steps. Part XVI §32's advantage over Part XVI §27 is a transport fact.

§42 Choosing an inference method

In order, and the first question is not about algorithms

Is it exact? Compute the treewidth. Chains, trees and linear-Gaussian systems admit exact inference, and using an approximation on one of them is a mistake people make because the approximate tools are better advertised.

Is it conjugate? A surprising number of small models have closed-form posteriors, and the closed form beats every method in this part.

How much does calibration matter? If a decision hangs on the width of an interval, the overconfidence Figure 4 measures disqualifies mean field, and you want sampling or a full-covariance family.

How many times will you infer? Once, expensively: sample. Millions of times at serving latency: amortise, and pay §22's gap.

Can you check? Simulate data from your own model with parameters you chose, and see whether the method recovers them. This is the single most useful thing in the movement and it is routinely skipped.

§43 What to report, and where this connects

Six things to report

Which method, and its family or proposal. "Variational" without the family is not a description.

Effective sample size, not the number of draws, and the Monte Carlo standard error computed from it.

How the chains were initialised. Figure 6 is why: R̂ from chains started together is uninformative.

A recovery check on simulated data with known parameters.

For a variational result, that the intervals are lower bounds on the true width.

For a diffusion model, the solver and the step count, since Figure 7 shows those changing the answer.

§44 What is unsolved

Four, distinguished by kind

Not yet measured. How large the amortisation gap is in the large models that use one. It is measurable (optimise the variational parameters per point and compare) and is rarely reported.

Not currently measurable. Whether a sampler has found all the modes of a high-dimensional posterior. Figure 6 shows the diagnostic cannot tell you, and no other statistic can either.

Genuinely open. Inference in models with discrete latent variables. The reparameterisation trick does not apply, every estimator trades bias against variance badly, and this is why so many models are continuous that would naturally be discrete.

Not empirical. Whether a prior is right. It is a claim about the world, and no amount of data settles it: only sensitivity analysis, which tells you whether it mattered.

§45 What to carry forward

Five things

A model is a factorisation, and the graph tells you the cost before the data arrives. Treewidth decides whether exact inference exists; everything else in this part is what you do when it does not.

Conditioning creates dependence as well as destroying it. Figure 1 measures a collider turning +0.004 into −0.758, which is why adjusting for more variables is not safer.

Variational inference is biased toward confidence, and compute does not fix it. Figure 4 measures a standard deviation of 0.436 against a true 1.000, exactly $\sqrt{1-\rho^2}$, with the optimiser working perfectly.

Sampling is exact in the limit and silent about whether you are there. Figure 6 measures a perfect R̂ = 1.0000 on a run that missed half the distribution.

Diffusion is a stochastic differential equation, its reverse needs only the score, and the same marginals admit a deterministic sampler that is invertible, exactly likelihood-bearing, and, with a first-order solver, measurably less accurate.

Sixty seconds

Writing down a joint distribution is easy and using one is not, because the sums are exponential. Everything here is a way round that. Structure: if the model factorises into small pieces the sums distribute and the posterior is exact, which covers chains, trees and linear-Gaussian systems and nothing much else. Optimisation: pick a family you can compute with and find its closest member, which is fast, is what a VAE does, and is reliably overconfident by an amount you can derive. Sampling: build a random walk whose long-run distribution is the posterior, which is right in the limit and cannot tell you when the limit has arrived. And in continuous time the same machinery becomes the language of diffusion models, where reversing a noising process needs one quantity, the gradient of the log density, and a network is trained to supply it.
