Read this first

There is no Chapter 1.5 in this book. This one exists because the book's Part I opens by assuming a comfort with mathematics that most readers last had in a classroom some years ago, and being rusty is not the same as being unable.

§0.1 Who this is for

You want to learn deep learning. You know there is mathematics in the way. Somewhere between "I did calculus once" and "I could not tell you what a derivative is right now" lies the truth, and you would rather fix that than keep bluffing past it.

That is a completely ordinary place to start. Mathematical fluency decays without use, exactly like a language; and like a language, it comes back far faster the second time than it went in the first. You are not relearning this from nothing. You are reactivating something.

So this document is unusually explicit. It does not skip steps on the grounds that they are obvious, because "obvious" is precisely what erodes first. Where a line of algebra has three steps, all three are written down.

What "getting your math back" actually requires

Two things, and only one of them is knowledge.

Recognition: seeing $\frac{\partial L}{\partial w}$ and knowing what kind of object it is, without a jolt of panic. This comes from exposure, and it comes quickly.

Fluency: being able to do it: rearrange the equation, apply the chain rule, take the derivative without looking anything up. This comes only from working problems, and there is no shortcut anyone has ever found.

Reading this document gives you the first. The 38 drills give you the second. If you only do one, do the drills.

§0.2 What is actually required

Less than you fear. Deep learning uses a narrow slice of mathematics, very heavily. It is not a broad survey; it is five or six ideas applied relentlessly.

Ranked by how much they matter:

IdeaWhy it mattersSection
The chain ruleBackpropagation is the chain rule. Nothing else comes close in importance.§17
LogarithmsEvery loss function lives in log space. Every one.§10–11
Partial derivatives and gradientsHow a model learns which way to adjust each of its millions of parameters.§22–23
ExponentialsSigmoid, softmax, Gaussians: all built from $e^x$.§9
Function compositionA deep network is a composition of functions. That is what "deep" means.§7
Summation notationYou cannot read a single formula without it.§12
Optimisation basicsTraining is minimising a function. Minima are where the derivative vanishes.§19–20
IntegralsNeeded to read Part II. You will never compute one by hand.§25
TrigonometryBarely. A page, for completeness.§13

Notice what is not there: no integration techniques, no trigonometric identities, no series convergence, no epsilon-delta proofs, no differential equations. A traditional calculus sequence spends most of its time on material deep learning never touches. This document does not.

§0.3 How to use it

Three honest routes, depending on where you are:

Badly rusty: "I could not define a derivative right now"
Read straight through, do every Try it, then all Tier 1 and Tier 2 drills. Budget two or three weeks at a relaxed pace. Do not skip Part B; the algebra is what everything else stands on.
Moderately rusty: "I remember calculus, vaguely"
Skim Parts B and C, slow down at Part D (logarithms), read Part F and §17 with full attention, then work Parts H–I properly. About a week.
Solid but never applied it to ML
Read §11, §17, §18, and Parts I and K. Everything else you already have. An afternoon.
The one section nobody should skip

§17, the chain rule. If you take nothing else from this document, take that section and its drills. Backpropagation (the algorithm that makes deep learning possible at all) is the chain rule applied repeatedly and organised carefully. Everything in Parts VII and IX of this book depends on it. It gets more room here than any other topic, deliberately.

§0.4 The markers

Intuition

The picture in your head: what a definition is really saying.

Pitfall

A specific error that is easy to make. Most of these are things that were drilled out of you once and drift back when you stop practising.

Why deep learning cares

Where this shows up later. Included so that you never work through something without knowing what it buys you.

Try it

Stop and compute. Answers come immediately after; but write yours down first, on paper. Reading an answer feels like understanding and is not.

In the framework

How the idea just derived appears in PyTorch, and what the library is doing underneath. Never shown before it has been built from scratch first.

There is one of these in Part 0, at the very end, and it exists to prove that the training step you will compute by hand in §24 is the same arithmetic a framework performs. Everything else here is pen and paper. They become common from Part I onward.
