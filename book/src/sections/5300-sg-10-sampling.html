Part AWhat a sample throws away

Everything in this book has been discrete: pixels, tokens, time steps. Each of them is a measurement of something continuous, taken at intervals, and there is an exact theory of what survives that operation and what does not. The theory is short, and one of its consequences is a defect in the convolutional architecture of Part X.

§1 Continuous and discrete

The objects

A signal is a function of a continuous variable: time for audio, two spatial coordinates for an image. Sampling evaluates it on a regular grid, giving the array your code actually holds. The sampling rate $f_s$ is how many samples per unit; its reciprocal is the spacing.

Two things then need saying, and only the second is surprising. Sampling loses information, and everyone expects that. But under a stated condition it loses nothing (the continuous signal can be reconstructed exactly from the samples), and when the condition fails, what you lose is not detail. It is worse than that.

§2 The sampling theorem

Go and read

Shannon (1949), Communication in the presence of noise. Read §II for the statement and the reconstruction formula proved below. It is a few pages of unusually clear prose, and the rest of the paper founds a different subject, which is worth the detour.

Nyquist–Shannon

If a signal contains no frequency above $B$, it is determined completely by samples taken at a rate $f_s > 2B$. The threshold $f_s/2$ is the Nyquist frequency: the highest frequency a given sampling rate can represent.

The reconstruction is explicit: each sample is multiplied by a $\operatorname{sinc}$ centred on it and the results are summed, which is what makes "loses nothing" a theorem and not a slogan.

Why 2B, in one picture

A sinusoid at frequency $f$ completes one cycle in $1/f$. To know a cycle happened at all you need at least one sample going up and one coming down, two per cycle. With fewer, the samples are consistent with a slower sinusoid, and nothing in the data distinguishes the two.

That last clause is the whole of the next section.

§3 Aliasing

What happens above Nyquist

Aliasing is the appearance of a frequency above $f_s/2$ as a different, lower frequency in the samples. A component at $f$ arrives indistinguishable from one at $|f - k f_s|$ for the integer $k$ that brings it into range.

The word to hold on to is indistinguishable. High-frequency content is not attenuated, not blurred, not lost. It is relabelled as low-frequency content and added to whatever was legitimately there, and no later processing can separate them. This is why the wheel in a film turns backwards, why a striped shirt shimmers on video, and why the fix has to happen before the samples are taken.

Pitfall: the repair only works beforehand

Once the samples exist, the aliased energy sits at the same coordinates as real signal. There is no filter, no network and no amount of training that undoes it, because the information needed to separate the two was destroyed by the sampling.

Which makes this one of the few problems in this book where the entire remedy is a decision made upstream: remove the content above Nyquist before sampling, not after.

§4 Where this bites in a network

Every stride is a resampling

A stride-2 convolution, or a pooling layer, keeps every other value of a feature map. That is sampling at half the rate, and the theorem applies verbatim: whatever the feature map contains above its new Nyquist frequency comes back as something else.

Feature maps are full of content above that limit. A ReLU is a nonlinearity, and nonlinearities create harmonics, a fact easy to state and easy to forget. So the classical design, convolution followed by stride, samples a signal that was never band-limited.

Part X §9 measured convolution's translation equivariance at float32 rounding and called it exact. It is, for the convolution. The next section measures what the downsampling does to the same guarantee.

§5 Measured

What this is and is not

It is not a bug in the implementation. The convolutions are exact and the pooling is exact. Shifting the input by one pixel changes which pixels the stride keeps, and the kept pixels differ because the feature map has content the halved rate cannot represent.

It is not small. Five orders of magnitude above the rounding error that Part X was content to call zero, and it grows with depth because each stage aliases what the previous one produced.

It is the mechanism behind a familiar observation (that image classifiers change their answer when the input shifts by a pixel), which is usually filed under robustness. It is a sampling error, and it has a textbook remedy.

§6 Anti-aliasing, and why it is not free

Go and read

Zhang (2019), Making convolutional networks shift-invariant again. Anti-aliased downsampling put into a real architecture. Read it for what the contribution actually was: the diagnosis was sixty years old and nobody had applied it. That is a category of available result, not a one-off.

Blur, then subsample

The remedy is the theorem read backwards: attenuate everything above the new Nyquist frequency before keeping every other sample. In a network that is a low-pass filter (a small blur) inserted before each stride.

What it costs. A real filter has a transition band, so it removes some signal below the cutoff and leaves some above it, which is why Figure 1's green curve falls by a factor of twelve rather than to zero. It also costs a convolution per stage.

What it buys. Shift-consistency, which is worth more than it sounds: a system whose answer depends on a one-pixel offset is a system whose evaluation has a variance term nobody measured.

Try this: on a model you have

Take any image classifier. Feed it an image, then the same image shifted by one pixel, and compare the output probabilities. Do it for shifts one through eight and record the range.

The number is usually larger than people expect, and it is measured in the same units as the accuracy differences that get published.

§7 What movement A establishes

Three things

Sampling is lossless under a condition and catastrophic outside it. Above Nyquist, content is not lost but relabelled, and relabelled content cannot be recovered.

Every stride in a network is a resampling of a feature map that nonlinearities have filled with high frequencies. The architecture of Part X samples a signal that was never band-limited.

The cost is measurable and the remedy is old. Figure 1 puts the shift-inconsistency five orders of magnitude above float32 rounding, and a low-pass filter before each stride removes most of it.
