PracticeDrills

Thirty-nine drills. The harness in parts/27-signals/drills/ covers twenty of them with thirty-two tests. Numpy only, and the transform is written out: an FFT you have built and checked against the definition is worth more than one you have imported.

Tier 0 Estimate first

EstimateE1 · The cost of a spectrogram

Ten seconds of stereo audio at 48 kHz in float32, analysed with a 1,024-sample window and a 256-sample hop. Write down the raw size and the number of transforms.

Answer. 3.84 MB and 3,744 transforms, 1,872 frames per channel. At about 104 operations each that is 4×107 operations, which is nothing: the front end is never the bottleneck, and the reason to think about it is Figure 5's resolution trade rather than its cost. Score yourself: within a factor of two is a pass, and an order of magnitude out is worth understanding before you go on.

Tier 1 Warm-up

Warm-upW1 · The rate

A signal contains content up to 8 kHz. What is the minimum sample rate?

Answer. Just above 16 kHz. Anything less and the content above half the rate does not vanish: it comes back as a lower frequency you cannot separate from real signal.
Warm-upW2 · Lost or relabelled

What exactly happens to a 12 kHz tone sampled at 16 kHz?

Answer. It arrives as 4 kHz. Not attenuated, not blurred: indistinguishable from a genuine 4 kHz tone, which is why no later processing recovers it.
Warm-upW3 · Read Figure 1

How far does a one-pixel shift move a pooled feature after four stride-2 stages, and how does that compare with Part X §9?

Answer. 3.34×10⁻², against float32 rounding at 1.19×10⁻⁷. Part X measured convolution and called it exact. It is; the downsampling is not.
Warm-upW4 · Why sinusoids

What property picks this basis rather than any other?

Answer. A shift leaves a sinusoid's frequency alone and changes only its phase. No other family does that, so it is the eigenbasis of every shift-invariant operator, the same symmetry argument as Part XXVI, with translation instead of permutation.
Warm-upW5 · The cost of the definition

What does the DFT cost computed directly, and what does the FFT cost?

Answer. O(n²) as a matrix product against O(n log n) by divide-and-conquer. Figure 2 measures the ratio growing to 28.5× at n = 8,192 with no sign of stopping.
Warm-upW6 · The theorem

State the convolution theorem and say what it makes of a filter.

Answer. Convolution in one domain is pointwise multiplication in the other. So a filter is a per-frequency scalar: the sinusoids are its eigenvectors and the frequency response is the list of eigenvalues.
Warm-upW7 · Which half matters

Magnitude or phase, which carries the image?

Answer. Phase. Figure 4 measures a reconstruction correlating +0.736 with its phase donor and −0.125 with its magnitude donor. An edge is many frequencies whose crests align, and the alignment is phase.
Warm-upW8 · Read Figure 5

What happens to the product of time and frequency resolution as the window grows?

Answer. Nothing: 1.875 at all three windows, while each factor moves sixteen-fold. Δf goes as fs/W and Δt as W/fs, so the window cancels exactly.
Warm-upW9 · Two spectra

How do natural images and white noise differ in Figure 6?

Answer. A power law at f⁻²·⁹¹ against a flat f⁰·⁰⁰. Natural data has almost all its energy at low frequency, which is what every smoothness prior in this book is betting on.
Warm-upW10 · Which first

A network fits a sum of sinusoids. In what order?

Answer. Low frequency first. Figure 7 measures 50 steps for f = 1 and never, within 20,000, for f = 16, and giving f = 16 four times the amplitude does not change the order.

Tier 2 Core

CoreC1 · Write the DFT

Build the transform matrix and apply it. Then verify it against a known input.

Answer. W[k,j] = exp(−2πijk/n), and X = Wx. A pure tone landing exactly on a bin should give exactly one non-zero magnitude; anything else means a normalisation or windowing error you have just found.
CoreC2 · Write the FFT

Implement radix-2 Cooley–Tukey and check it against C1.

Answer. Bit-reverse, then log₂n butterfly stages. Agreement to about 10⁻¹³ relative in float64, not exactly zero, because the naive route accumulates its own error over n terms.
CoreC3 · Parseval

Check that energy is conserved, then break it on purpose.

Answer. Σ|x|² = (1/n)Σ|X|². Drop the 1/n and the check fails by exactly n, which is the point: a normalisation bug does not throw and does not look wrong, and this is the one line that catches it.
CoreC4 · Round-trip

Transform and invert a real signal. What should the imaginary part be?

Answer. At rounding level. If it is not, your spectrum is not conjugate-symmetric and something upstream corrupted it.
CoreC5 · Reproduce aliasing

Sample a sinusoid above Nyquist and identify the frequency you get.

Answer. |f − k·fs| for the k that brings it into range. Confirm by transforming the samples and reading off the peak: it is a single clean peak in the wrong place, not a smear.
CoreC6 · The shift test

Write a check that a downsampling stack is shift-invariant, and watch it fail.

Answer. Pool the output, shift the input by one, compare. Three lines, and it is Part XXVI §1's permutation test in the translation group. It produced Figure 1.
CoreC7 · Fix it

Add a low-pass filter before each stride and re-run C6.

Answer. The inconsistency drops by about an order of magnitude and does not vanish, because a small binomial kernel is not an ideal filter. Figure 1 measures 11.7× at four stages.
CoreC8 · Convolve two ways

Convolve directly and through the transform, and time both against kernel length.

Answer. Identical to machine precision, and the costs cross: Figure 3 puts it at k = 256 for a signal of 2¹⁶. Vision kernels sit three orders of magnitude below that, which is why nobody does this in a conv net and everybody does it in a long-convolution model.
CoreC9 · Zero-padding is not free

FFT convolution of two length-n signals without padding. What goes wrong?

Answer. You get circular convolution: the tail wraps onto the head. Pad to at least n + k − 1. This is the same wrap-around that makes Figure 1's experiment exact, used deliberately there and a bug here.
CoreC10 · Leakage

Transform a tone at a frequency between two bins, with and without a window.

Answer. Unwindowed, the peak grows skirts across every bin: the DFT's periodicity assumption meeting a discontinuous seam. A Hann window widens the main lobe and drops the skirts by orders of magnitude.
CoreC11 · Build the four filters

Construct low-pass, high-pass and band-pass kernels and verify each by its frequency response. Then check the centred difference [−1, 0, 1].

Answer. Transform the kernel and look. The one to check is the last: [−1, 0, 1] is exactly zero at DC and exactly zero at Nyquist, peaking in between. It is band-pass, so the derivative kernel inside every Sobel operator is blind to the alternating pattern a high-pass filter is meant to catch. The binomial kernels have the mirror property (an exact null at Nyquist) which is what makes them anti-aliasers.
CoreC12 · Swap magnitude and phase

Reproduce Figure 4 with two images of your own.

Answer. The result looks like the phase donor every time. Then check the correlations rather than trusting your eyes, because "looks like" is the claim being tested.
CoreC13 · The uncertainty product

Measure time and frequency resolution across window lengths.

Answer. The product is constant, and exactly so, not approximately, because both widths scale linearly with the window in opposite directions. There is no window that is good at both.
CoreC14 · Build a spectrogram

Write the short-time transform and sweep the window length on a signal with both a tone and a transient.

Answer. The tone sharpens and the transient smears, or the reverse. Every spectrogram in every paper is a choice on this curve, and it is usually not reported.
CoreC15 · The graph Laplacian

Compute L = D − A for a path graph and look at its eigenvectors.

Answer. They are cosines. The path graph's Laplacian is the second-difference operator, so its eigenbasis is the Fourier basis, which is what §25 means by saying frequency is an eigenbasis rather than a fact about waves.
CoreC16 · Over-smoothing in the eigenbasis

Express Part XXVI's message passing as an operation on Laplacian eigenvectors.

Answer. It scales each eigenvector by a factor below one, more severely at high frequency, so repeated application drives everything toward the lowest mode. Over-smoothing is a low-pass filter applied too many times.
CoreC17 · Measure a spectrum, and check the estimator

Fit a power-law exponent to natural images, and first to a synthetic image whose exponent you set.

Answer. Calibrate before you quote. Figure 6 recovers −1.98 and −2.94 for true −2 and −3, which is what makes its measured −2.91 a statement about the images rather than about the code.
CoreC18 · Downsample carelessly

Reduce an image fourfold with and without filtering, and compare spectra.

Answer. The unfiltered version carries about three times the power across the top half of the resulting band. The discarded frequencies did not leave; they were relabelled and added to what was there.
CoreC19 · Reproduce spectral bias

Fit a network to a sum of sinusoids and track each component.

Answer. Strictly in frequency order, and the highest components may never arrive. Then swap the amplitudes to confirm it is frequency and not loudness that decides.
CoreC20 · Fourier features

Re-run C19 with the input replaced by a bank of sines and cosines.

Answer. The high frequencies arrive. Then push the bank's bandwidth up until the network fits noise immediately, which is the honest description of the trick: the cutoff moved from the optimiser to you.

Tier 3 Stretch

StretchS1 · Audit a model you have

Take a trained image classifier and measure how its output moves under shifts of one to eight pixels.

Answer. Usually more than expected, and in the same units as published accuracy differences. Then check whether the architecture filters before its strides. Most do not.
StretchS2 · Anti-alias a real network

Insert blur-before-stride throughout a small classifier, retrain, and measure both accuracy and shift-consistency.

Answer. Consistency improves substantially. Whether accuracy does is the measurement, and it is the part §44 says is under-reported.
StretchS3 · Positional encoding as a bank

Plot the sinusoidal encoding's frequencies and verify that a shift acts linearly on it.

Answer. Each sine–cosine pair rotates by an angle depending only on the shift, so relative position is a fixed linear map. That property is the entire design rationale, and it is checkable in five lines.
StretchS4 · Extend a context window

Implement rotary embeddings, then position interpolation, then frequency-dependent scaling, and compare on sequences longer than the training length.

Answer. Uniform scaling costs local discrimination; scaling only the low frequencies does not, because the fast components were fully trained and were doing a different job.
StretchS5 · A learned front end

Train a model on a mel spectrogram and one on raw samples with a learned first layer, and compare both accuracy and cost.

Answer. Often close, sometimes better, at more compute, and what the learned filters converge to is worth looking at, because it is frequently something like a filterbank.
StretchS6 · Phase in a loss

Train an audio model against a magnitude-only spectral loss and listen to the output.

Answer. It scores well and sounds wrong. The loss cannot see the quantity §18 measures as carrying the structure, which is why phase reconstruction is its own subject.
StretchS7 · A long-convolution layer

Implement a sequence layer whose kernel is as long as the sequence, computed through the FFT.

Answer. Affordable only through the transform, which is Figure 3's right-hand branch and the reason state space models are practical at all.
StretchS8 · Find the resampling in a pipeline you run

Trace every resize, stride, pool and rate change in a system you use, and check which of them filters first.

Answer. There are usually more than expected (image loading, augmentation, the model, and often the evaluation) and the ones in the data pipeline are the least likely to filter.
